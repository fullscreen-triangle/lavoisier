\section{A Single Claim}
\label{sec:ch2-claim}

The theoretical framework of this monograph rests on one empirical observation.
Not a postulate in the sense of something assumed for convenience; not a
principle in the sense of something deduced from deeper structure.
An observation, elevated to axiomatic status because of the universality and
reproducibility of the evidence.

Every persistent physical system ever measured occupies a bounded region of
phase space.

Planets stay in their orbits.
Atoms persist for geological epochs.
Ions survive transit through a mass spectrometer.
Not one observed system has ever escaped to infinity in phase space and kept
going.
The experimental record is unanimous.

We formalise this observation as the Bounded Phase Space Law.

%% ── §2.2 ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{The Axiom}
\label{sec:ch2-axiom}

Let $(\Omega, \mathcal{B}, \mu)$ be a measure space where $\Omega$ is the
phase space of a physical system, $\mathcal{B}$ is its Borel $\sigma$-algebra,
and $\mu$ is the Liouville measure.
Let $\phi_t : \Omega \to \Omega$ be the one-parameter flow of the dynamics.

\begin{axiom}{Bounded Phase Space Law (BPSL)}{bpsl}
Every persistent dynamical system satisfies:
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
  \item \textbf{Boundedness.}
    $\mu(\Omega) < \infty$.
    The phase space has finite Liouville measure.
  \item \textbf{Measure preservation.}
    $\phi_t$ is measure-preserving: $\mu(\phi_t^{-1}(A)) = \mu(A)$ for all
    $A \in \mathcal{B}$ and all $t \geq 0$.
  \item \textbf{Hierarchical partitionability.}
    There exists a sequence of measurable partitions
    $\Partition_1 \prec \Partition_2 \prec \cdots \prec \Partition_n \prec \cdots$
    of $\Omega$ such that each $\Partition_{n+1}$ is a refinement of
    $\Partition_n$ and the partition algebra
    $\mathcal{A}_\infty = \bigvee_{n \geq 1} \mathcal{A}(\Partition_n)$
    is dense in $\mathcal{B}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{axiom}

Each clause carries precise physical content.

\paragraph{Clause (i): Boundedness.}
This is the source of Poincaré recurrence.
A system with infinite phase-space volume is not guaranteed to return; a system
with finite volume must.
Without clause (i), persistence cannot be defined.

\paragraph{Clause (ii): Measure preservation.}
This is Liouville's theorem: the Hamiltonian flow preserves phase-space volume.
It is the source of the unitary structure of quantum mechanics: the Koopman
operator $U_t f = f \circ \phi_t$ on $\Hilbert = L^2(\Omega, \mu)$ is
unitary whenever $\phi_t$ is measure-preserving.
Without clause (ii), the spectral theory that generates the quantum numbers
would not be well-posed.

\paragraph{Clause (iii): Hierarchical partitionability.}
This is the structural claim that the phase space can be observed at
increasingly fine resolution.
It is the source of the quantum numbers, the shell capacity, the selection
rules, and the charge quantisation.
Without clause (iii), measurement would have no structure.

Taken together, the three clauses extract the minimal structure of
``a persistent physical system that can be observed''.
Remove any one clause, and either persistence, observability, or structure
fails.

\section{On the Empirical Status of the Axiom}
\label{sec:ch2-empirical}

A theoretical axiom is a statement posited for its deductive consequences,
with empirical support that is indirect.
An empirical axiom is a statement that is itself an observation, elevated to
axiomatic status because of its universality.

The Bounded Phase Space Law is empirical in the second sense.
It makes no claim about \emph{why} persistent systems are bounded.
It states only that they are.

This logical choice mirrors the foundational choices of every major physical
theory.
Classical mechanics takes Newton's laws.
Special relativity takes the constancy of $c$.
Quantum mechanics takes the Born rule.
Thermodynamics takes the three laws.
In each case the axiomatic starting point is an empirical observation.
What distinguishes the Bounded Phase Space Law is its generality: it is
implied by each of these theories and is simpler than any of them.

\begin{remark}[What the Axiom does not assume]
  The Axiom does not assume:
  \begin{itemize}
    \item The existence of a Hilbert space, a wavefunction, or the Born rule.
    \item Newton's equations of motion or the Euler--Lagrange equations.
    \item The Lorentz group, the light postulate, or the equivalence principle.
    \item Maxwell's equations or the gauge principle.
    \item The standard model symmetry group.
  \end{itemize}
  All of these are derived as Consequences in the chapters that follow.
\end{remark}

\section{Mathematical Prerequisites}
\label{sec:ch2-math}

The derivations that follow use standard results from five areas of mathematics.
We state the key tools here; proofs may be found in the standard references.

\paragraph{Ergodic theory.}
\textit{Birkhoff's ergodic theorem}: for any $f \in L^1(\Omega, \mu)$, the
time average $\lim_{T\to\infty} \frac{1}{T}\int_0^T f(\phi_t(x))\,dt$
converges $\mu$-almost everywhere.
\textit{Poincaré's recurrence theorem}: for any set $A$ of positive measure,
$\mu$-almost every point of $A$ returns to $A$ infinitely often.

\paragraph{Spectral theory.}
The Koopman operator $U_t$ on $\Hilbert = L^2(\Omega,\mu)$ is unitary.
Its generator $H$ (defined by $U_t = e^{-itH}$) is self-adjoint.
If $\Omega$ is compact, $H$ has discrete spectrum $\{\omega_k\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}$.

\paragraph{Representation theory.}
The rotation group $SO(3)$ has irreducible representations labelled by
$l \in \mathbb{N}_0$, of dimension $2l+1$.
Its double cover $SU(2)$ has additional half-integer representations ($s=\half$).
The fundamental group $\pi_1(SO(3)) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ supplies the
spin quantum number.

\paragraph{Fourier analysis on bounded domains.}
A function on a bounded domain has a discrete Fourier series, not a continuous
Fourier integral.
The orthonormal basis is countable.

\paragraph{Measure theory.}
The partition algebra generated by a nested sequence of measurable partitions
is separating (clause~iii) if and only if the sequence generates the Borel
$\sigma$-algebra.
The Shannon entropy of a partition $\Partition = \{A_i\}$ is
$H(\Partition) = -\sum_i \mu(A_i)\log\mu(A_i)$.

\section{The Axiom as Generator of Observability}
\label{sec:ch2-observability}

There is a consequence of Axiom~\ref{ax:bpsl} that is immediately important for
the mass spectrometric application of this monograph: observability is a
theorem, not an assumption.

In traditional formulations of quantum mechanics, the measurement process is
added as a separate postulate (the collapse postulate).
In the present framework, measurement emerges from clause~(iii): a
\emph{categorical observation} is the identification of the partition cell
$A \in \Partition_n$ occupied by the system at partition depth $n$.
The observer's finite information capacity limits how deep in the partition
hierarchy they can resolve.
But the structure of what can be resolved --- the cell lattice, the refinement
order, the entropy of the partition --- is given by the Axiom.

The practical consequence for mass spectrometry: an ion analyser is a
partition-depth resolver.
The Orbitrap, FT-ICR, TOF, and quadrupole each resolve the ion's partition
depth to different precision; higher-resolution instruments resolve deeper
into the partition hierarchy.
The mass measurement \emph{is} a partition-depth measurement.
This identification is made precise in Part~VIII.

\section{Organisation of the Consequences}
\label{sec:ch2-organisation}

The remainder of Parts~II through~VII derives the physical content of the
Axiom systematically.
Each result is labelled a ``Consequence'' rather than a ``Theorem'', following
the terminology of the source derivation~\cite{Sachikonye2024BPS}:
the results are not discoveries but the logical content of the Axiom made
explicit.

The deductive structure is shown in full in \Cref{app:flowchart}.
Every Consequence is at distance at most five from the Axiom:
the longest chain of logical dependencies is five steps.
The derivation never introduces an additional postulate.
