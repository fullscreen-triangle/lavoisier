\section{A Single Claim}
\label{sec:ch2-claim}

The method of this monograph is narrow and strict.
One empirical observation is promoted to the status of an axiom; every
subsequent statement is either a definition, a formal consequence of the axiom
proved in full, or the citation of a measurement compared against such a
consequence.
No statement of physical substance appears without one of these three
justifications.

The single observation is this: every persistent physical system that has ever
been observed occupies a bounded region of its phase space and admits a
hierarchical partition into distinguishable sub-regions.
We formalise this in \cref{sec:ch2-axiom} as the \emph{Bounded Phase Space Law}
(BPSL).
The Law is empirical.
Its status here is identical to the status that, for example, the equivalence
principle enjoys in general relativity, or that the homogeneity of space
enjoys in the derivation of the Galilei group: an empirical summary to which
the rest of the development is logically subordinate.

What follows the Law is deduction.
The preceding chapter showed one ion --- $m/z = 299.0555$ --- described
classically and quantum-mechanically on four different instruments, agreeing to
within two parts per million each time.
The rest of this monograph answers why that agreement is necessary, and what
else must be true alongside it.

\section{The Deductive Protocol}
\label{sec:ch2-protocol}

Before stating the axiom, we state the method by which it is used.
This matters because the logical form of the argument is unusual: a single
empirical axiom is the source of every quantitative result, with no additional
physical input.

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=crownblue!5, colframe=crownblue!60!black,
                  title={\textbf{Deductive Protocol}}]
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
  \item State the Axiom formally (\cref{sec:ch2-axiom}).
  \item For each subsequent claim $C_i$, prove $C_i$ as a Consequence using
    only:
    \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
      \item the Axiom;
      \item prior Consequences explicitly cited by label;
      \item standard mathematical results from cited references.
    \end{enumerate}
  \item Conclude each proof with $\square$.
  \item Sequester physical interpretation in Remark paragraphs that do not
    enter the proofs.
  \item For each Consequence involving a quantitative prediction, compare the
    prediction to a measurement in \cref{ch:predictions}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{tcolorbox}

We adhere to this protocol throughout.
Any Remark can be excised without affecting the logical integrity of the
derivation.
Any numerical comparison in \cref{ch:predictions} is independent of every
other comparison.

The practical implication: a reader who rejects a Consequence in,
say, \cref{ch:mass} must either exhibit an error in the proof of that
Consequence or reject the Axiom itself.
There is no third option.
Rejecting the Axiom is possible only by producing a persistent physical system
that violates its content.
No such system has ever been observed.

\section{Empirical Grounding of the Axiom}
\label{sec:ch2-empirical}

The Axiom is defended by measurement, not by argument.
We list representative observations drawn from distinct regimes of physics
and scales of length.
Each observation is a statement that a persistent physical system occupies a
bounded region of its phase space; the list covers twenty-five orders of
magnitude in spatial scale and fifteen orders of magnitude in temporal scale.

\paragraph{Electrons in atoms.}
Ionisation energies of hydrogen-like species lie between $13.6$\,eV (neutral
hydrogen) and many keV for inner-shell electrons of heavy elements.
The existence of a positive ionisation threshold \emph{is} the statement that
the electronic region of phase space is bounded; an electron with energy less
than the threshold cannot escape.
Hydrogen atoms in a discharge lamp radiate for arbitrarily many cycles without
loss of boundedness of the orbit.

\paragraph{Atoms in molecules.}
Molecular dissociation energies range from $\sim 0.1$\,eV (van der Waals
dimers) to $\sim 11$\,eV (the triple-bonded CO molecule).
The existence of a finite dissociation energy is the statement that the
vibrational-rotational phase space of the molecule is bounded.

\paragraph{Atoms and molecules in condensed matter.}
Cohesive energies of solids lie between $\sim 0.01$\,eV (van der Waals solids)
and $\sim 10$\,eV (covalent and ionic crystals).
Every condensed-matter structural measurement presupposes that the atoms'
positions are confined to a bounded region of configuration space.

\paragraph{Photons in optical cavities.}
Finesse values exceeding $10^{6}$ in high-$Q$ Fabry--Pérot cavities correspond
to photon confinement lifetimes of milliseconds to seconds, during which a
single photon executes $10^{6}$ or more round-trip reflections without escape.
The photon's phase space in the cavity is bounded.

\paragraph{Gases in containers.}
That gas molecules remain inside any container whose walls are impermeable is
an observation of such ubiquity that it is rarely listed as an observation at
all; it is nevertheless the statement that the configurational phase space of
each gas molecule is bounded by the container walls.

\paragraph{Nucleons in nuclei.}
Nuclear binding energies of $\sim 8$\,MeV per nucleon across the periodic
table are the statement that the nucleon's phase space inside a nucleus is
bounded by a potential well of depth comparable to $8$\,MeV per particle.

\paragraph{Stars in galaxies and galaxies in clusters.}
Stars are observed to orbit the centres of galaxies with typical speeds of
$10^{2}$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ over timescales of $10^{9}$ years without escape.
Galaxies orbit cluster potentials over similar fractional timescales.
Each such observation is the statement that the gravitational phase space is
bounded.

\paragraph{Particles in storage rings.}
At accelerator facilities, charged particles are circulated in storage rings
for hours, completing $10^{10}$ to $10^{12}$ revolutions in a bounded
transverse phase space.
Beam loss is measured, quantified, and minimised; a persistent beam is by
definition one whose phase space remains bounded.

\medskip\noindent
In each regime, the observation of persistence \emph{is} the observation of
phase-space boundedness.
We are not aware of any persistent physical system whose phase space has been
observed or measured to be unbounded.
The physical notion of a \emph{dispersing} system --- a system whose phase-space
region grows without bound --- is precisely the complement of persistence.

The partitionability clause of the Axiom is also empirical.
All measurement apparatuses distinguish finitely (or countably) many outcomes;
that fact is what makes measurement itself a coherent operation.
The existence of spectroscopic lines, of atomic and nuclear energy levels, of
diffraction orders, of discrete charge states, and of identifiable molecular
isomers, is the observational statement that the relevant phase spaces admit
partitions into distinguishable sub-regions.
A continuous (unpartitioned) state space admits no finite-resolution measurement
and no reproducible classification.

We therefore treat the Axiom not as a proposal to be defended but as a datum.
Its defence is the whole of observational physics to date.

\begin{remark}[What counts as a counter-observation]
  A counter-observation to the Axiom would be: a persistent physical system
  whose measured phase-space region has infinite Liouville measure.
  ``Measured'' is the crucial qualifier.
  A conjectured system (e.g., an isolated particle in an unbounded universe)
  is not a counter-observation unless the unboundedness is measured.
  ``Persistent'' means: observed to maintain its identity over a macroscopic
  fraction of its natural timescale.
  A particle observed for one transit time through a detector is not persistent
  in this sense; an atom observed for $10^{8}$ oscillation periods is.
  No such counter-observation has been reported in any empirical physics journal
  of the last 150 years, to our knowledge.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Why the Liouville measure]
  The choice of Liouville measure $\mu = \prod_i dq_i\,dp_i$ in clause~(i)
  is forced by the requirement of measure preservation under a Hamiltonian flow
  (Liouville's theorem); any other measure would be modified by the flow.
  Boundedness under the Liouville measure is the precise formalisation of
  ``bounded accessible phase-space region''.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Why hierarchical partitioning]
  The hierarchical partitioning of clause~(iii) is the requirement that
  measurements produce distinguishable outcomes at multiple resolutions.
  A measurement apparatus of finite resolution distinguishes cells of some
  partition $\Partition_n$; a more precise apparatus distinguishes cells of a
  refined partition $\Partition_{n+1}$.
  The hierarchy extends this to a sequence of refinements, with the total
  $\sigma$-algebra generated being the Borel algebra, so every measurable
  observable is expressible as a limit of partition cells.
  This structure is not a postulate about the universe; it is a statement about
  the category of observations.
  If measurements produced continuous rather than categorical outcomes, no
  finite-precision experiment could ever be reproducible --- a conclusion
  contradicted by daily experimental practice.
\end{remark}

\section{Formal Statement of the Axiom}
\label{sec:ch2-axiom}

Let $(\Omega, \mathcal{B}, \mu)$ be a measure space where $\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2d}$
is an open bounded subset of phase space (with conjugate coordinate pairs
$(q_i, p_i)$, $i = 1,\ldots,d$), $\mathcal{B}$ is the Borel $\sigma$-algebra
of $\Omega$, and $\mu$ is the Liouville measure $\mu = \prod_i dq_i\,dp_i$.
Let $\phi_t : \Omega \to \Omega$ denote a one-parameter family of measurable
maps indexed by $t \in \mathbb{R}$, interpreted as the flow of a dynamical
system.

\begin{axiom}{Bounded Phase Space Law (BPSL)}{bpsl}
Every \emph{persistent} dynamical system $(\Omega, \mu, \phi_t)$ satisfies:
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
  \item \textbf{Boundedness.}
    $0 < \mu(\Omega) < \infty$.
    The phase space has finite positive Liouville measure.
  \item \textbf{Measure preservation.}
    $\phi_t$ is measure-preserving: for every $t \in \mathbb{R}$ and every
    $A \in \mathcal{B}$, $\mu(\phi_t^{-1}(A)) = \mu(A)$.
  \item \textbf{Hierarchical partitionability.}
    There exists a sequence of finite measurable partitions
    $\Partition_0 \preceq \Partition_1 \preceq \Partition_2 \preceq \cdots$
    of $\Omega$, with each $\Partition_{n+1}$ a refinement of $\Partition_n$
    such that each cell of $\Partition_n$ has positive measure and the
    generated $\sigma$-algebra $\bigvee_n \mathcal{A}(\Partition_n)$ equals
    $\mathcal{B}$ modulo $\mu$-null sets.
\end{enumerate}
\end{axiom}

The word \emph{persistent} means: observed to exist over a macroscopic range
of times relative to the natural dynamical timescale.
An atom is persistent over its radiative lifetime; a nucleon over its
confinement timescale; a galaxy over its dynamical time.

\paragraph{Clause~(i): Boundedness.}
This is the source of Poincaré recurrence.
A system with infinite phase-space volume is not guaranteed to return;
a system with finite positive volume must (\cref{ch:oscillation}).
Without clause~(i), persistence cannot be defined.

\paragraph{Clause~(ii): Measure preservation.}
This is Liouville's theorem: the Hamiltonian flow preserves phase-space volume.
It is the source of the unitary structure of quantum mechanics: the Koopman
operator $U_t f = f \circ \phi_t$ on $\Hilbert = L^2(\Omega, \mu)$ is
unitary whenever $\phi_t$ is measure-preserving.
Without clause~(ii), the spectral theory that generates the quantum numbers
would not be well-posed.

\paragraph{Clause~(iii): Hierarchical partitionability.}
This is the structural claim that the phase space can be observed at
increasingly fine resolution.
It is the source of the quantum numbers, the shell capacity, the selection
rules, and the charge quantisation (Chapters~\ref{ch:coordinates}
and~\ref{ch:shells}).
Without clause~(iii), measurement would have no structure.

\bigskip
Taken together, the three clauses extract the minimal structure of
``a persistent physical system that can be observed''.
Remove any one clause, and either persistence, observability, or structure
fails.

\subsection{Remarks on the Formal Statement}

\paragraph{Dimensionality.}
We write $\Omega \subset \mathbb{R}^{2d}$ where $d$ is the number of spatial
dimensions.
For most physical applications $d = 3$.
The clauses of the Axiom are agnostic about $d$; they hold for any finite $d$.
The emergence of three spatial dimensions as a Consequence of the Axiom --- not
an input to it --- is proved in \cref{ch:coordinates}.

\paragraph{Smoothness of the flow.}
The flow $\phi_t$ is assumed measurable.
Typically in physical applications it is smooth (a Hamiltonian solution), but
the Axiom does not require smoothness; only measurability and measure
preservation.
Smoothness is assumed additionally in Consequences that invoke the
Euler--Lagrange formalism (\cref{ch:lagrangian}).

\paragraph{Continuous vs.\ discrete time.}
The flow $\phi_t$ is parametrised by $t \in \mathbb{R}$.
For discrete-time systems ($t \in \mathbb{Z}$), the Axiom and Consequences
carry over with appropriate modifications; the Koopman operator becomes a
unitary power rather than a unitary group, but the spectral theory is unchanged.

\paragraph{Classical vs.\ quantum formulations.}
We state the Axiom in classical language.
Its equivalent quantum formulation: replace $\Omega$ by the Hilbert space of
states, $\mu$ by the trace class of positive unit-trace operators, and
$\phi_t$ by the Schrödinger flow.
The two formulations are equivalent up to the Koopman embedding.
Our development from the classical side is a choice of presentation, not a
commitment to classical over quantum mechanics.

\paragraph{Relation to stochastic processes.}
If $\phi_t$ is stochastic, the Axiom generalises to require that the
transition kernel $P_t(x, \cdot)$ preserves $\mu$.
The Consequences below mostly carry over; modifications are outside the scope
of this monograph.

\section{The Attack Surface}
\label{sec:ch2-attack}

Because the monograph asserts exactly one substantive claim, the set of
legitimate criticisms is narrow.
We enumerate it explicitly.

\begin{enumerate}[label=(A\arabic*)]
  \item \textbf{Exhibit a persistent unbounded system.}
    \label{at:unbounded}
    A reader who observes a persistent physical system with $\mu(\Omega) = \infty$
    falsifies clause~(i).
    No such observation is known.

  \item \textbf{Exhibit a persistent non-measure-preserving system.}
    \label{at:nonliouville}
    A reader who observes a persistent microscopic dynamics that is not
    measure-preserving falsifies clause~(ii).
    No such observation is known.

  \item \textbf{Exhibit a persistent non-partitionable system.}
    \label{at:nonpartition}
    A reader who observes a persistent physical system whose state space admits
    no nested measurable partition (a continuous state space without any
    finite-resolution distinguishable classification) falsifies clause~(iii).
    No such observation is known; all measurements produce countable outcomes.

  \item \textbf{Identify a logical error in a proof.}
    \label{at:proof}
    A reader who finds a specific step in one of the proofs below that does
    not follow from the cited prior results and standard mathematics falsifies
    the deduction at that step.
\end{enumerate}

Any criticism of this monograph that is not of type (A1)--(A4) is not a
logical criticism of the framework; it is a dispute about interpretation, and
interpretation is sequestered to Remark paragraphs that do not enter the proofs.

\begin{remark}[Implicit assumptions]
  The proofs below also rely on standard results of real analysis, measure
  theory, functional analysis, ergodic theory, representation theory, and
  algebraic topology.
  These are not axioms of this monograph; they are shared mathematical
  infrastructure, cited where invoked.
  A reader who rejects Birkhoff's ergodic theorem or Stone's theorem for
  one-parameter unitary groups is not criticising this framework but disputing
  a century of mathematical results.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Physical language in formal proofs]
  Several sections use physical language for exposition (``oscillation'',
  ``mass'', ``charge'', ``atom'').
  In each case the corresponding mathematical object is defined formally before
  the language is invoked, and the physical interpretation is sequestered to
  Remark or Interpretation paragraphs.
  The reader who wishes to verify the logic without physical background can
  ignore the Remarks and read only the formal environments (Definition, Lemma,
  Proposition, Corollary, Consequence, Axiom).
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Notation collision]
  We use $\mu$ for the Liouville measure and also for the inertial mass
  parameter in the partition Lagrangian (\cref{ch:lagrangian}).
  The context disambiguates: $\mu(A)$ with an argument is always the measure;
  $\mu$ in the Lagrangian is always the mass parameter.
  Similarly, $n$ is the principal depth coordinate and also the number of modes
  in various counting arguments; the context disambiguates.
\end{remark}

\section{Mathematical Prerequisites}
\label{sec:ch2-math}

The derivations that follow use standard results from five areas of mathematics.
We state the key tools here without proof; these are invoked by citation in
subsequent Consequences.

\paragraph{Ergodic theory.}
\textit{Birkhoff's ergodic theorem}: for any $f \in L^1(\Omega, \mu)$, the
time average $\lim_{T\to\infty} \frac{1}{T}\int_0^T f(\phi_t(x))\,dt$
converges $\mu$-almost everywhere to a $\phi_t$-invariant function.
\textit{Poincaré's recurrence theorem}: for any measurable set $A$ with
$\mu(A) > 0$, $\mu$-almost every point of $A$ returns to $A$ infinitely often
under a measure-preserving flow on a finite-measure space.

\paragraph{Spectral theory.}
The Koopman operator $U_t$ on $\Hilbert = L^2(\Omega,\mu)$ defined by
$(U_t f)(x) = f(\phi_t(x))$ is unitary when $\phi_t$ is measure-preserving.
By Stone's theorem, $U_t = e^{-\iu t H}$ for a self-adjoint generator $H$.
If $\Omega$ is compact, the resolvent of $H$ is compact and the spectrum is
pure point: $\mathrm{spec}(H) = \{\omega_k\}_{k \in \mathbb{N}}$ discrete.

\paragraph{Representation theory.}
The rotation group $SO(3)$ has irreducible representations labelled by
$l \in \mathbb{N}_0$, of dimension $2l+1$.
Its double cover $SU(2)$ has additional half-integer representations
($s = \half$).
The fundamental group $\pi_1(SO(3)) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ supplies the
spin quantum number.

\paragraph{Fourier analysis on bounded domains.}
A function on a bounded domain has a discrete Fourier series, not a continuous
Fourier integral.
The orthonormal eigenbasis of $H$ on a bounded domain is countable; the
spectrum is discrete.

\paragraph{Measure theory.}
The partition algebra generated by a nested sequence of measurable partitions
is separating (clause~iii) if and only if the sequence generates the Borel
$\sigma$-algebra.
The Shannon entropy of a partition $\Partition = \{A_i\}$ is
$H(\Partition) = -\sum_i \mu(A_i)\log\mu(A_i)$.
Standard results in descriptive measure theory show that a partition hierarchy
generating $\mathcal{B}$ exists whenever $(\Omega, \mathcal{B}, \mu)$ is a
Lebesgue space.

\section{The Axiom as Generator of Observability}
\label{sec:ch2-observability}

There is an immediate consequence of Axiom~\ref{ax:bpsl} that is important for
the mass spectrometric application of this monograph: observability is a
theorem, not an assumption.

In traditional formulations of quantum mechanics, the measurement process is
added as a separate postulate (the collapse postulate).
In the present framework, measurement emerges from clause~(iii): a
\emph{categorical observation} is the identification of the partition cell
$A \in \Partition_n$ occupied by the system at partition depth $n$.
The observer's finite information capacity limits how deep in the partition
hierarchy they can resolve.
But the structure of what can be resolved --- the cell lattice, the refinement
order, the entropy of the partition --- is given by the Axiom.

For mass spectrometry: an ion analyser is a partition-depth resolver.
The Orbitrap, FT-ICR, TOF, and quadrupole each resolve the ion's partition
depth to different precision; higher-resolution instruments resolve deeper into
the partition hierarchy.
The mass measurement \emph{is} a partition-depth measurement.
This identification is made precise in Chapters~\ref{ch:forcefree}
and~\ref{ch:gpu}.

\begin{consequence}{Observability from the Axiom}{observability}
Let $(\Omega, \mu, \phi_t)$ satisfy Axiom~\ref{ax:bpsl}.
Then for every $n \geq 1$, the partition $\Partition_n$ from clause~(iii)
defines a measurable categorical observation: the map
$\mathrm{obs}_n : \Omega \to \Partition_n$ assigning to each $x \in \Omega$ the
unique cell of $\Partition_n$ containing $x$ is measurable, takes countably
many values, and the algebra $\mathcal{A}(\Partition_n)$ it generates is a
sub-$\sigma$-algebra of $\mathcal{B}$.
As $n \to \infty$, the observations $\mathrm{obs}_n$ separate points of $\Omega$
modulo $\mu$-null sets.
\end{consequence}

\begin{proof}
Each cell $A_{n,j} \in \Partition_n$ is measurable by clause~(iii).
The map $\mathrm{obs}_n(x) = j$ iff $x \in A_{n,j}$ is measurable since each
preimage $\mathrm{obs}_n^{-1}(\{j\}) = A_{n,j} \in \mathcal{B}$.
The algebra generated is $\mathcal{A}(\Partition_n) \subset \mathcal{B}$.
Separation: for $x \neq y$ with $\mu(\{x\}) = 0$, there exists $n$ large
enough that $x$ and $y$ lie in different cells of $\Partition_n$, because
$\bigvee_n \mathcal{A}(\Partition_n) = \mathcal{B}$ modulo null sets.
\end{proof}

\section{The Axiom Does Not Assume}
\label{sec:ch2-doesnot}

Before listing what the Axiom generates, it is useful to list what it does
not assume.

\begin{remark}[What the Axiom does not assume]
  The Axiom does not assume:
  \begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item The existence of a Hilbert space, a wavefunction, or the Born rule.
    \item Newton's equations of motion or the Euler--Lagrange equations.
    \item The Lorentz group, the light postulate, or the equivalence principle.
    \item Maxwell's equations or the gauge principle.
    \item The standard model symmetry group $SU(3)\times SU(2)\times U(1)$.
    \item The ideal gas law $PV = NkT$.
    \item The three laws of thermodynamics.
    \item The Pauli exclusion principle.
    \item The shell structure of atoms.
    \item Newton's gravitational constant $G$.
  \end{itemize}
  All of these are derived as Consequences in the chapters that follow.
\end{remark}

\section{Organisation of the Consequences}
\label{sec:ch2-organisation}

The remainder of Parts~II through~VIII derives the physical content of the
Axiom systematically.
Each result is labelled a ``Consequence'' rather than a ``Theorem'': the
results are not discoveries but the logical content of the Axiom made
explicit.

The deductive structure is shown in full in \cref{app:flowchart}.
Every Consequence is at distance at most five from the Axiom: the longest
chain of logical dependencies is five steps.
The derivation never introduces an additional postulate.

The structure of the derivation:
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
  \item \textbf{Part~II} (\cref{ch:oscillation,ch:coordinates}):
    Oscillatory necessity, discrete spectrum, $E = \hbar\omega$, partition
    coordinates $(n,l,m,s)$, shell capacity $C(n) = 2n^2$, selection rules,
    Pauli exclusion, compression cost, three-dimensional space.
  \item \textbf{Part~III} (\cref{ch:lagrangian,ch:lorentz,ch:mass,ch:charge}):
    Partition Lagrangian, propagation bound $c$, Lorentz invariance, rest mass
    $m_0 = \hbar\omega_0/c^2$, $E = mc^2$, charge emergence.
  \item \textbf{Part~IV} (\cref{ch:shells,ch:atomic,ch:nuclear,ch:subnucleon}):
    Coulomb envelope, Rydberg formula, Aufbau principle, shielding, magic
    numbers, nucleon structure, sub-nucleon confinement.
  \item \textbf{Part~V} (\cref{ch:sentropy,ch:floor,ch:triple}):
    S-entropy triple $(\Sk, \St, \Se)$, Floor Theorem, Triple Equivalence
    $\Osc \cong \Cat \cong \Part$.
  \item \textbf{Part~VI} (\cref{ch:idealgas,ch:transport,ch:electrical}):
    Ideal gas law, Maxwell--Boltzmann, universal transport, partition
    extinction, superconductivity, Wiedemann--Franz.
  \item \textbf{Part~VII} (\cref{ch:inflation,ch:gravity}):
    Composition inflation $T(n,d) = d(d+1)^{n-1}$, three independent routes
    to Newton's constant $G$, MOND, dark energy, dark matter ratio.
  \item \textbf{Part~VIII} (\cref{ch:forcefree,ch:gpu,ch:database,ch:purpose}):
    Force-free ion, GPU as physical instrument, generative spectral database
    $O(k)$, purpose-based analysis, molecular Maxwell Demon.
\end{itemize}
