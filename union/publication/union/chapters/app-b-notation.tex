\subsection*{Labelling conventions}

Throughout the monograph, equations are referenced with
``\texttt{(n.m)}'' (English-prefixed numbers).  Consequences are labelled
with a \texttt{cons:} prefix for cross-referencing; Lemmas with
\texttt{lem:}; Propositions with \texttt{prop:}; Corollaries with
\texttt{cor:}.  Axiom, Consequence, and Definition environments are
typeset with coloured tcolorbox frames.  Remark and Interpretation
paragraphs are sequestered from proofs; they do not enter any logical chain.

\section*{Glossary of Non-Standard Terms}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Glossary}
% ============================================================

The following terms are non-standard or are given specialised meanings
within the bounded phase space framework.  Their formal definitions appear
in the indicated chapters; the glossary entries give informal explanations
sufficient for general comprehension.

\begin{description}[leftmargin=0pt, itemsep=6pt]

  \item[Partition depth.]
    The nesting level in the hierarchy of Axiom~(iii); equals the principal
    quantum number $n$ for electronic and nuclear states.  A system at
    partition depth $n$ occupies a cell at the $n$-th level of the
    partition refinement, analogously to an electron in the $n$-th shell.
    Introduced: Ch.~\ref{ch:axiom}. Formal definition: Definition~\ref{def:principal}.

  \item[Partition cell.]
    A measurable sub-region of $\Omega$ at a given partition depth; the
    finest distinguishable region at that depth.  The analogue of a quantum
    eigenstate.  Two cells at the same depth are distinguishable if and only
    if their partition coordinates $(n,l,m,s)$ differ.
    Introduced: Ch.~\ref{ch:axiom}.

  \item[Partition boundary.]
    The zero-measure set separating two adjacent partition cells of positive
    measure.  The locus of electric charge (Ch.~\ref{ch:charge}): by
    Consequence~\ref{cons:chargeemergence}, the observable charge of a system
    is a property of this boundary rather than of the interior.

  \item[Categorical observation.]
    A measurement outcome that identifies the partition cell occupied by the
    system at a given depth.  The partition-framework analogue of quantum
    measurement.  By Consequence~\ref{cons:categorical}, a finite-information
    observer can resolve at most one cell per measurement; continuous
    observation is impossible within the framework.

  \item[Non-actualisation.]
    The set of partition cells a system has \emph{not} visited up to time
    $t$: $\mathcal{U}(t)=\mathcal{A}_{\infty}\setminus\mathcal{V}(t)$.  By
    Consequence~\ref{cons:massmemory}, the rate of accumulation of unvisited
    cells is the physical origin of rest mass; by
    Consequence~\ref{cons:loschmidt}, the non-actualisation set is
    monotone non-decreasing, which is the physical origin of the arrow of
    time.

  \item[Partition extinction.]
    The coalescence of distinct partition cells into a single cell under
    phase-locking conditions at $T<T_{c}$.  When this occurs, all transport
    coefficients associated with the merged partition vanish identically
    (Consequence~\ref{cons:partitionextinction}).  The mechanism of
    superconductivity (electron-pair partition extinction), superfluidity
    (bosonic partition extinction), and Bose--Einstein condensation.

  \item[Virtual substate.]
    An intermediate sub-coordinate in the Fisher-metric embedding of the
    partition hierarchy (Consequence~\ref{cons:virtualsubstates}) that lies
    outside any observable partition cell but is necessary for efficient
    navigation of the partition space.  The partition-framework realisation
    of quarks and gluons: they are virtual substates, not global states,
    and therefore cannot be isolated (confinement).
    Introduced: Ch.~\ref{ch:shells}.

  \item[Path opacity.]
    The principle that sub-coordinate paths through the Fisher-metric
    embedding are not distinguishable in the final global state
    (Consequence~\ref{cons:virtualsubstates}).  Two paths through the
    sub-coordinate space that terminate at the same global partition cell
    produce identical observational outcomes.  This is the structural
    mechanism of quark confinement: the specific quark-configuration path
    within a hadron is not observable; only the hadronic global state is.

  \item[Composition inflation.]
    The exponential growth of categorically distinguishable trajectories
    with partition depth: $T(n,d)=d(d+1)^{n-1}$
    (Consequence~\ref{cons:compositioninflation}).  For $d=3$ and
    $n\approx 56$, $T$ exceeds the number of Planck cells in the observable
    universe, defining the trans-Planckian regime.

  \item[Triple Equivalence.]
    The categorical isomorphism $\Osc\cong\Cat\cong\Part$
    (Consequence~\ref{cons:tripleent}): the oscillatory description of a
    bounded system (classical mechanics) is formally equivalent to its
    categorical description (information theory) and its partition
    description (quantum mechanics).  No three distinct theories are
    present; they are three representations of the same structure.

  \item[S-entropy.]
    Distance-to-truth on the bounded scale $[0,100]$ relative to a receiver
    $R$ and question $q$; axiomatised in Ch.~\ref{ch:sentropy} independently
    of Shannon entropy and Boltzmann entropy.  The triple equivalence
    establishes that $\Sk=\St=\Se$ for any bounded system.

  \item[Partition Lagrangian.]
    The unique Galilean-invariant Lagrangian coupling a particle to the
    partition depth field $\Depth(\mathbf{x},t)$ and vector potential
    $\Apartition(\mathbf{x},t)$:
    $\Lagr=\tfrac{1}{2}\mu|\dot{\mathbf{x}}|^{2}+\mu\dot{\mathbf{x}}\cdot\Apartition-\Depth$.
    Not postulated; derived in Ch.~\ref{ch:lagrangian} by completeness of
    scalar-plus-vector coupling of a particle to a field in the
    non-relativistic limit.

  \item[Categorical temperature.]
    $T_{\text{cat}}=T_{\text{phys}}/\Depth$; the effective temperature of a
    single-particle gas when the partition depth acts as a degeneracy factor
    (Consequence~\ref{cons:singleparticle}).  For a heavy ion of depth
    $\Depth\sim 10^{6}$ at 77 K, $T_{\text{cat}}\sim 38.5\,\mu$K.

  \item[Partition lag time.]
    $\taup$; the minimum temporal resolution of the partition hierarchy.
    Equals $\hbar/E$ where $E$ is the transition energy.  The propagation
    bound satisfies $c=\Delta x/\taup$ exactly.

\end{description}

\section*{Further Commentary on Select Consequences}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Commentary on Select Consequences}

We offer additional commentary on three Consequences that have been the
subject of historical debate or conceptual difficulty.

\subsection*{On Consequence~\ref{cons:loschmidt} and the Arrow of Time}

The Loschmidt paradox observes that microscopic dynamics is time-symmetric
(invariant under $t\to -t$), yet macroscopic thermodynamics shows a
definite arrow of time.  Classical resolutions invoke coarse-graining, the
statistical mixing of phase-space trajectories, or the expanding
cosmological boundary condition.

Consequence~\ref{cons:loschmidt} resolves the paradox structurally.  The
microscopic dynamics is indeed time-symmetric, but the \emph{identity} of a
state is defined by the set of partition cells it has visited, not by its
instantaneous phase-space position.  The set of visited cells
$\mathcal{V}(t)$ can only grow, not shrink, because visitation is a
monotonic operation: once a cell has been visited, it remains in the
history.  This monotonicity is not a statistical fact; it is a
set-theoretic fact about accumulated history.  The arrow of time is a
consequence of the structure of the partition history, not of the dynamics.

This framing explains why entropy increase is observed even for systems far
from thermodynamic equilibrium: it is not thermalisation that drives entropy
increase; it is the accumulation of history.  The result holds at the level
of a single particle: a single Koopman mode, once excited, contributes its
cell to the visited set forever, independently of whether equilibrium is
reached.

\subsection*{On Consequence~\ref{cons:chargeemergence} and the Concept of Field}

The conventional electromagnetic field is often spoken of as
``real'': a physical entity pervading space, capable of carrying energy and
momentum, mediating interactions between charges.
Consequence~\ref{cons:chargeemergence} reframes this: charge is a property
of the partition boundary; the ``field'' is the gradient of the partition
depth outside the boundary.

Under this reframing, the field is not an entity separate from the charges
that generate it.  It is the spatial extension of the partition structure
of which the charges are boundary attributes.  The field does not
``exist'' at points where no partition boundary is in causal contact; it
emerges only in regions accessible to a bounded chain of partition
boundaries.

This reframing is compatible with all experimental observations of the
electromagnetic field.  The field is real in the sense that its gradient
drives physical motion; it is not real in the sense of being an independent
substance.  This distinction matters for conceptual clarity, not for
numerical predictions.

\subsection*{On Consequence~\ref{cons:subnucleon} and Confinement}

The confinement of quarks within hadrons is a well-established empirical
fact but is notoriously difficult to derive from the first-principles
standard-model Lagrangian (QCD).  Lattice QCD calculations exhibit
confinement numerically but do not provide a simple analytic derivation.

Consequence~\ref{cons:subnucleon} provides a structural derivation:
quark-like constituents are virtual substates in the partition hierarchy's
sub-coordinate decomposition.  By Consequence~\ref{cons:virtualsubstates},
these sub-coordinates are path-opaque; they cannot be isolated as global
states.  The confinement is therefore not a dynamical fact to be derived
from the Lagrangian; it is a structural fact about the partition hierarchy.

QCD's dynamical derivation of confinement (non-Abelian gauge-theory
asymptotic freedom, colour-charge screening) is the computational
realisation of this structural fact.  Both statements are correct; they are
complementary levels of description.

\section*{Response to Anticipated Objections}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Response to Objections}
% ============================================================

\subsection*{``The Axiom is trivial''}

An objection: ``boundedness is so obviously true that it cannot support a
significant derivation.''

Response: the Axiom is indeed empirically self-evident, but its deductive
consequences are far from trivial.  From boundedness alone this monograph
derives Lorentz invariance, quantum discreteness, charge quantisation, the
magic numbers, and more.  The non-triviality of the derivations, not of
the axiom, is the content of the monograph.

A more sophisticated version of the same objection: ``everyone already
knows that boundedness implies discreteness, so this paper contains nothing
new.''

Response: the implication from boundedness to discreteness
(Consequence~\ref{cons:discretemodes}) is indeed part of standard functional
analysis.  What is new here is the systematic application of such
implications across a wide range of physical phenomena, always from the
same starting point, and the combination of these implications to derive
structures --- like the Rutherford cross-section, the magic numbers, or the
Aufbau principle --- that were historically obtained from different starting
points.  Originality resides in the synthesis.

\subsection*{``The proofs rely on standard results''}

An objection: ``the proofs use Koopman's theorem, the Ignatowsky
derivation, Slater's rules.  These are not original results.''

Response: correct.  The monograph's contribution is not in deriving new
mathematical theorems but in identifying a single empirical observation
from which many existing theorems follow by standard methods.  The
contribution is the synthesis and the identification of the minimal starting
point, not the lemmas.

\subsection*{``The framework does not predict new physics''}

An objection: ``this paper re-derives what is already known but makes no
novel predictions.''

Response: the framework's predictive content is the Axiom itself.  Any
future observation that contradicts a Consequence falsifies the Axiom.
Predictions include: no continuous-charge observations; no persistent
unbounded systems; no violation of Lorentz invariance; no magic numbers
outside the derived set $\{2,8,20,28,50,82,126\}$; no free sub-nucleon
constituents.  If any of these fails, the framework fails.  The framework
thus makes the strongest possible predictive claim: the universe agrees with
boundedness, or the Axiom is falsified.

Additionally, the conceptual reframing of charge, mass, and confinement in
structural-topological terms suggests specific computational approaches to
physical problems that may not be obvious from the standard-model
perspective.  These are open research directions (virtual multi-modal
spectrometry, categorical chromatography, partition-coordinate database
indexing).

\subsection*{``The philosophical interpretation is contentious''}

An objection: ``the framing of mass as non-actualisation, or charge as a
boundary attribute, departs from standard physical intuition.''

Response: the framing is indeed non-standard, but it is pedagogical rather
than essential.  The mathematical Consequences are independent of the
framing; one can accept the theorems without accepting the interpretation.
The interpretation is sequestered to Remarks and Interpretations paragraphs,
as explicitly stated in the protocol section; a reader who rejects the
interpretation need not reject the results.

\subsection*{``The Axiom is too general to be useful''}

An objection: ``anything this general must be either wrong or vacuous.''

Response: the Axiom is demonstrably neither.  It is not vacuous because it
has numerous non-trivial consequences that agree with measurement.  It is
not wrong because no measurement contradicts it.  The generality of the
Axiom is paid for by the strictness of its empirical support: every
persistent system ever measured has been bounded.

The generality is, in fact, the source of the framework's parsimony.  A
more specific Axiom would have a narrower scope; this one covers 24 orders
of magnitude of empirical physics with a single starting point.

\section*{On Style and the Term ``Consequence''}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Stylistic Note}
% ============================================================

A stylistic comment on the monograph's terminology.  The environment name
``Consequence'' is used throughout in place of the more common
``Theorem''.  The choice is not superficial.

A \emph{theorem} is a statement one \emph{proves}; the implication is that
the theorem represents a discovery or a novel result.  A
\emph{consequence} is a statement that \emph{follows}; the implication is
that the result is unavoidable once the premises are granted.  Our
Consequences are the latter: they are what the Axiom entails.  They are not
discoveries or novel results in the ordinary sense; they are the logical
content of the Axiom made explicit.

This choice of terminology reflects the monograph's rhetorical strategy.  We
are not advancing a new theory; we are cataloguing what an empirical
observation logically requires.  A critic cannot legitimately attack a
Consequence's content (it is a theorem of the stated premises); they can
only attack the Axiom or the proof.  This narrows the attack surface.

A secondary stylistic choice: physical interpretation is kept separate from
formal proofs.  Every Remark and Interpretation paragraph is sequestered;
none enters a proof.  A reader interested purely in the mathematical content
can read only the Definitions, Lemmas, Propositions, Corollaries,
Consequences, and their proofs, ignoring all Remarks.  The logical structure
stands or falls with the formal content alone.
