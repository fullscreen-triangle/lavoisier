\section{Floor Positivity}
\label{sec:ch14-floor}

The third axiom of the S-functional (Axiom~(S3) of Chapter~\ref{ch:sentropy})
asserts that the infimum of all S-values attainable by a bounded receiver is
strictly positive.  We now derive this positivity as a theorem from the
cardinality structure of bounded receivers, thereby giving the floor its
mathematical substance.

\begin{theorem}[Floor Positivity]\label{thm:floor-pos-mono}
For every bounded receiver $\mathcal{R}$, the floor satisfies
\[
  S_{\flat}(\mathcal{R}) \;>\; 0.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Suppose for contradiction that $S_{\flat}(\mathcal{R}) = 0$.  By Axiom~(S3)
there is a candidate $x_0 \in \mathcal{X}$ with
$S_{\mathcal{R}}(x_0, x^{*}) = 0$.  By Axiom~(S4) this requires
\[
  \delta_{\mathcal{R}}\!\bigl(\Phi_{\mathcal{R}}(\sigma_{x_0}),\;
  \Phi_{\mathcal{R}}(\sigma_{x^{*}})\bigr) = 0.
\]
But $\delta_{\mathcal{R}}(\sigma, \sigma') = 0$ implies $\sigma = \sigma'$:
since $\delta_{\mathcal{R}}(\sigma, \sigma) = S_{\flat}(\mathcal{R}) = 0$
and $\delta_{\mathcal{R}}$ is non-decreasing with floor on the diagonal,
$\delta_{\mathcal{R}}(\sigma, \sigma') < S_{\flat}(\mathcal{R})$ is impossible
unless the arguments coincide.  Therefore
$\Phi_{\mathcal{R}}(\sigma_{x_0}) = \Phi_{\mathcal{R}}(\sigma_{x^{*}})$
in $K_{\mathcal{R}}$.

By the Residue Persistence Theorem (Theorem~\ref{thm:residue-mono}), since
$|K_{\mathcal{R}}| < |\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{tot}}|$, the preimage
$\mathrm{dec}_{\mathcal{R}}^{-1}(\{x^{*}\})$ has cardinality at most
$|K_{\mathcal{R}}|$, while $\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{tot}}$ has strictly greater
cardinality.  Therefore the cognitive residue has positive measure with respect
to any cardinality-preserving measure on $\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{tot}}$.  This
contradicts the assumption that the residue's information content vanishes in
the floor.  Hence $S_{\flat}(\mathcal{R}) > 0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Smallest Knowable Error]\label{cor:smallest-knowable-mono}
For every bounded receiver $\mathcal{R}$ and every truth
$x^{*} \in \mathcal{X}^{*}$, there is no candidate $x \in \mathcal{X}$
with $S_{\mathcal{R}}(x, x^{*}) < S_{\flat}(\mathcal{R})$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Immediate from Theorem~\ref{thm:floor-pos-mono} and the definition of the
infimum in Axiom~(S3).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The floor as a receiver-intrinsic constant]
The floor $S_{\flat}(\mathcal{R})$ is a property of the receiver alone: it
depends on the structure of $(\Sigma_{\mathcal{R}}, \Phi_{\mathcal{R}},
K_{\mathcal{R}}, \mathrm{dec}_{\mathcal{R}})$ but not on the candidate $x$
or the truth $x^{*}$.  Two distinct truths $x_1^{*}, x_2^{*} \in
\mathcal{X}^{*}$ can each be approached to the same floor, but no closer.
The floor therefore characterises the receiver's fundamental epistemic
limitation, not the difficulty of any particular problem.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Physical interpretation]
The Floor Theorem is the information-theoretic analogue of the Heisenberg
uncertainty principle: no receiver with finite information capacity can make
a perfectly exact identification.  It is, however, stronger than the
uncertainty principle in two respects.  First, it requires no quantum
mechanical framework---it holds for any bounded receiver, classical or quantum.
Second, the floor $S_{\flat}(\mathcal{R}) > 0$ is a strict lower bound on
distance-to-truth, not a lower bound on a product of uncertainties.  The
Heisenberg bound is a special case, arising when the receiver's knowledge
framework $K_{\mathcal{R}}$ is a Hilbert space and the S-functional is
computed from the Born rule.
\end{remark}

\section{The S-Expression Algebra}
\label{sec:ch14-expressions}

To state the Unconstrained Subtask Theorem precisely, we need the algebra of
S-expressions---the collection of syntactic objects on which receivers act.

\begin{definition}[Atomic expression]\label{def:atomic-mono}
An \emph{atomic expression} is an element of one of the three representation
spaces $\mathbf{Osc} \cup \mathbf{Cat} \cup \mathbf{Part}$, together with a
representation tag $R \in \{\mathfrak{O}, \mathfrak{E}, \mathfrak{P}\}$.
We write atomic expressions as pairs $(\alpha, R)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[S-expression]\label{def:s-expression-mono}
The set of \emph{S-expressions} $\mathcal{E}$ is the smallest set generated
by:
\begin{enumerate}[(1)]
  \item Every atomic expression $(\alpha, R)$ is in $\mathcal{E}$;
  \item Every real number $r \in \mathbb{R}$ is in $\mathcal{E}$
    (literal subtask);
  \item Every conversion $\Phi^{R \to R'}(\xi)$ for $\xi \in \mathcal{E}$
    and $R, R' \in \{\mathfrak{O}, \mathfrak{E}, \mathfrak{P}\}$ is in
    $\mathcal{E}$;
  \item Every binary composition $\xi_1 \star \xi_2$ for
    $\star \in \{+, -, \cdot, /, \oplus, \otimes\}$ and
    $\xi_1, \xi_2 \in \mathcal{E}$ is in $\mathcal{E}$;
  \item Every unary operation $u(\xi)$ for
    $u \in \{\mathrm{id}, -, (\cdot)^{-1}, \exp, \log\}$ and
    $\xi \in \mathcal{E}$ is in $\mathcal{E}$;
  \item Every recursive triple $(\xi_k, \xi_t, \xi_e)$ for
    $\xi_k, \xi_t, \xi_e \in \mathcal{E}$ is in $\mathcal{E}$.
\end{enumerate}
We write $\mathcal{E}^{(d)}$ for expressions of recursion depth at most $d$
and $\mathcal{E}^{*}$ for those of finite depth.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Receiver evaluation]\label{def:eval-mono}
For receiver $\mathcal{R}$, the \emph{receiver evaluation} is a partial map
\[
  \mathrm{eval}_{\mathcal{R}} : \mathcal{E} \rightharpoonup K_{\mathcal{R}}
\]
defined inductively: atomic expressions evaluate via the decoder; real literals
evaluate to $r \cdot e_{\mathcal{R}}$ (the unit of $K_{\mathcal{R}}$);
conversions evaluate via the conversion functor; binary and unary operations
evaluate receiver-internally; recursive triples evaluate componentwise.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Subtask]\label{def:subtask-mono}
A \emph{subtask} of an expression $\xi \in \mathcal{E}$ is any
sub-expression $\eta$ that occurs as an argument of a binary, unary,
conversion, or triple operation in the inductive construction of $\xi$.
We write $\mathcal{U}(\xi)$ for the set of subtasks of $\xi$.
\end{definition}

\section{The Unconstrained Subtask Theorem}
\label{sec:ch14-subtask}

\begin{theorem}[Unconstrained Subtask]\label{thm:unconstrained-mono}
Let $\xi \in \mathcal{E}$ be an expression with
$\mathrm{eval}_{\mathcal{R}}(\xi) = k^{*} \in K_{\mathcal{R}}$, and let
$S^{*} := S_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathrm{dec}_{\mathcal{R}}(k^{*}), x^{*})$
be the global S-value.  Then for any subtask $\eta \in \mathcal{U}(\xi)$,
the value of $S_{\mathcal{R}}(\eta, x^{*})$ is \textbf{unconstrained}: there
exist expressions $\xi'$ obtained from $\xi$ by replacing $\eta$ with arbitrary
$\eta' \in \mathcal{E}$ such that
$\mathrm{eval}_{\mathcal{R}}(\xi') = k^{*}$ and
$S_{\mathcal{R}}(\xi', x^{*}) = S^{*}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $\xi$ be parsed as $\xi = C[\eta]$ where $C[\cdot]$ is the surrounding
context (an expression with one hole).  For any $\eta'$ with
$\mathrm{eval}_{\mathcal{R}}(\eta') = \mathrm{eval}_{\mathcal{R}}(\eta)$,
the substitution $\xi' = C[\eta']$ satisfies
$\mathrm{eval}_{\mathcal{R}}(\xi') = k^{*}$ by induction on the size of $C$,
using that $\mathrm{eval}_{\mathcal{R}}$ is a homomorphism with respect to
the operations of $\mathcal{E}$.

The set of such $\eta'$ is large: it includes all expressions in any of the
three representations whose evaluation matches, all mixed-type expressions
whose conversions cancel, and all signed-fractional expressions with arithmetic
equivalence.

Crucially, $\eta'$ may have arbitrary $S_{\mathcal{R}}(\eta', x^{*})$:
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
  \item $\eta' = \eta + (1 - 1)$, with the literal $1$ at maximum local
    S-distance and $-1$ at maximum local S-distance, their sum contributing
    nothing;
  \item $\eta' = \Phi^{R \to R'}(\eta'')$ in a different representation, with
    the conversion-internal subtasks at any local S-value;
  \item $\eta' = \eta_1 + \eta_2$ with $\eta_1 = -\eta_2 + \eta$, so $\eta_1$
    and $\eta_2$ are individually at any preferred S-value.
\end{itemize}
The local S-value of the subtask is therefore \emph{not} constrained by the
global S-value of the whole expression.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Local Infeasibility is Global Feasibility]
\label{cor:local-infeas-mono}
There exist expressions $\xi$ with global S-value $S^{*}$ arbitrarily close
to $S_{\flat}(\mathcal{R})$ (near-perfect) in which one or more subtasks
have local S-value $100$ (maximally incorrect).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Take $\xi = \eta + (-\eta) + \xi_0$ where $\eta$ has S-value $100$ (maximally
wrong) and $\xi_0$ has S-value at floor.  By
Theorem~\ref{thm:unconstrained-mono}, the composition has the same global
S-value as $\xi_0$ alone.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Local-Global Decoupling]\label{thm:lg-decoupling-mono}
For any global S-value $S^{*} \in [S_{\flat}(\mathcal{R}), 100]$ and any
sequence of target local S-values
$(\sigma_1, \sigma_2, \ldots, \sigma_n) \in [S_{\flat}(\mathcal{R}), 100]^n$,
there exists an expression $\xi \in \mathcal{E}$ with subtasks
$\eta_1, \ldots, \eta_n$ satisfying simultaneously:
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
  \item $S_{\mathcal{R}}(\xi, x^{*}) = S^{*}$;
  \item $S_{\mathcal{R}}(\eta_i, x^{*}) = \sigma_i$ for each $i$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Construct
$\xi := \xi_0 + \sum_{i=1}^{n} (\eta_i - \eta_i)$
where $\xi_0$ is an expression with global S-value $S^{*}$ and each $\eta_i$
is chosen to have local S-value $\sigma_i$.  By
Theorem~\ref{thm:unconstrained-mono}, the $\eta_i$ are arbitrary subject to
their evaluations cancelling.  By Lemma~\ref{lem:image-S-mono}, every value
in $[S_{\flat}(\mathcal{R}), 100]$ is achievable as a local S-value.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[The Miracle Principle]\label{cor:miracle-mono}
For any global S-value $S^{*}$, there exist expressions $\xi$ realising
$S^{*}$ in which arbitrarily many subtasks have local S-value $100$
(locally maximally infeasible).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:lg-decoupling-mono} with $\sigma_i = 100$ for all
$i$ in any chosen subset.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Apples and oranges admissibility]
Theorem~\ref{thm:unconstrained-mono} formalises the ``apples-and-oranges''
principle.  An expression $p_1 + t_1 + 1$ where $p_1$ is nominally a position
and $t_1$ a time is admissible because the receiver's evaluation
$\mathrm{eval}_{\mathcal{R}}$ converts each to a common representation via
the functors of the Triple Equivalence (Chapter~\ref{ch:triple}) before
composition.  The local types are syntactic surface; the global S-value is
what is asserted.
\end{remark}

\section{Compositional Multiplicity}
\label{sec:ch14-multiplicity}

\begin{definition}[Composition]\label{def:composition-mono}
A \emph{composition} of an expression $\xi$ is a finite sequence of binary
operations $(\eta_1, \star_1, \eta_2, \star_2, \ldots, \eta_n)$ in
$\mathcal{E}$ such that
$\eta_1 \star_1 \eta_2 \star_2 \cdots \star_{n-1} \eta_n = \xi$
when evaluated under the receiver.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Composition Multiplicity]\label{thm:comp-mult-mono}
For any expression $\xi \in \mathcal{E}^{*}$ of finite depth, the number of
distinct compositions of $\xi$ is at least $2^{|\mathcal{U}(\xi)|-1}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For each subtask $\eta \in \mathcal{U}(\xi)$ that occurs as an argument of a
binary operation, swapping its role with its sibling produces a distinct
composition under non-commutative interpretations of operations.  By induction
on $|\mathcal{U}(\xi)|$, the number of compositions doubles at each binary
node, yielding the bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[Compositions of the integer $3$]
The expression $3 \in \mathcal{E}$ admits compositions including:
\begin{align*}
  3 &= 1 + 1 + 1, \quad 2 + 1 = 1 + 2, \quad 4 - 1,\\
  3 &= \tfrac{6}{2} = 3 \cdot 1 = \log_e e^3,\\
  3 &= \sin(3\pi/2) + 4, \quad 3\cdot\tfrac{4}{4}.
\end{align*}
All evaluate to $3$ under any arithmetically faithful receiver, confirming that
the global S-value is preserved across all syntactically distinct compositions.
\end{example}

\section{Information Catalysts}
\label{sec:ch14-catalysts}

A surprising consequence of the S-calculus is the existence of
\emph{information catalysts}---expressions whose application strictly decreases
the receiver's S-value, with multiplicative rather than additive benefit under
composition.

\begin{definition}[Catalyst]\label{def:catalyst-mono}
A \emph{catalyst} for receiver $\mathcal{R}$ at truth $x^{*}$ is an expression
$\gamma \in \mathcal{E}$ such that the catalytic application
$\xi \mapsto \xi \diamond \gamma$ reduces global S-value:
\[
  S_{\mathcal{R}}(\xi \diamond \gamma,\; x^{*})
  \;<\; S_{\mathcal{R}}(\xi,\; x^{*})
\]
for every $\xi$ with $S_{\mathcal{R}}(\xi, x^{*}) > S_{\flat}(\mathcal{R})$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Catalytic power]\label{def:catpower-mono}
The \emph{catalytic power} of catalyst $\gamma$ at receiver $\mathcal{R}$ on
truth $x^{*}$ is
\[
  \kappa_{\mathcal{R}, x^{*}}(\gamma)
  := \sup_{\xi \in \mathcal{E}}
  \frac{S_{\mathcal{R}}(\xi, x^{*})
        - S_{\mathcal{R}}(\xi \diamond \gamma, x^{*})}
       {S_{\mathcal{R}}(\xi, x^{*}) - S_{\flat}(\mathcal{R})}.
\]
The catalyst is \emph{strict} if $\kappa > 0$, \emph{absolute} if $\kappa = 1$,
and \emph{neutral} if $\kappa = 0$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Catalytic-power range]\label{lem:cat-power-range-mono}
For every catalyst $\gamma$:
$\kappa_{\mathcal{R}, x^{*}}(\gamma) \in [0,1]$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The numerator is bounded above by the denominator because S-values cannot
decrease below $S_{\flat}(\mathcal{R})$ (Corollary~\ref{cor:smallest-knowable-mono}).
\end{proof}

\section{Multiplicativity of Catalytic Power}
\label{sec:ch14-multiplicativity}

\begin{theorem}[Multiplicativity of Catalytic Power]
\label{thm:mult-cat-mono}
Let $\gamma_1, \gamma_2$ be catalysts for receiver $\mathcal{R}$ at truth
$x^{*}$ with catalytic powers $\kappa_1$ and $\kappa_2$ respectively.
Then the catalytic power of the composite $\gamma_1 \diamond \gamma_2$ is
\[
  \kappa_{\mathcal{R}, x^{*}}(\gamma_1 \diamond \gamma_2)
  \;=\; 1 - (1 - \kappa_1)(1 - \kappa_2).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Apply $\gamma_1$ first; the residual S-distance above floor is multiplied by
$(1 - \kappa_1)$.  Apply $\gamma_2$ to the result; the residual is further
multiplied by $(1 - \kappa_2)$.  The total fraction of distance closed is
$1 - (1 - \kappa_1)(1 - \kappa_2)$, which is the catalytic power of the
composite.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Diminishing Returns]\label{cor:diminishing-mono}
Repeated application of the same catalyst $\gamma$ a total of $n$ times yields
composite catalytic power
\[
  1 - (1 - \kappa)^n,
\]
which converges to $1$ as $n \to \infty$ but never reaches it.
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[Cascade Composition Rule]\label{cor:cascade-mono}
The catalytic power of a cascade $\Gamma = (\gamma_1, \ldots, \gamma_n)$
applied in right-associative order is
\[
  \kappa(\Gamma) = 1 - \prod_{i=1}^{n}(1 - \kappa_i).
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:mult-cat-mono} inductively.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Catalyst-Driven Convergence]\label{thm:cat-conv-mono}
For any $\xi_0 \in \mathcal{E}$ with
$S_{\mathcal{R}}(\xi_0, x^{*}) > S_{\flat}(\mathcal{R})$ and any catalyst
$\gamma$ with $\kappa := \kappa_{\mathcal{R}, x^{*}}(\gamma) > 0$, the
sequence
\[
  \xi_n := \xi_0 \diamond \gamma^n
\]
satisfies
\[
  S_{\mathcal{R}}(\xi_n, x^{*}) - S_{\flat}(\mathcal{R})
  \;\leq\; (1-\kappa)^n
  \bigl[S_{\mathcal{R}}(\xi_0, x^{*}) - S_{\flat}(\mathcal{R})\bigr].
\]
In particular, $S_{\mathcal{R}}(\xi_n, x^{*}) \to S_{\flat}(\mathcal{R})$
geometrically as $n \to \infty$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By induction on $n$ using Theorem~\ref{thm:mult-cat-mono}.  At each step,
the residual S-distance above floor is multiplied by $(1 - \kappa)$, giving
the geometric decay.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Linear-Time Convergence to Floor]
For any $\varepsilon > 0$, the number of catalyst applications required to
reach S-value within $\varepsilon$ of floor is
$O\!\bigl(\log(1/\varepsilon) / \log(1/(1-\kappa))\bigr)$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}[Perfect catalysts and the floor]
A catalyst with $\kappa = 1$ (absolute) reduces S-value to floor in a single
application.  Its existence requires that the receiver have access to a
perfectly-aligned decoder for the truth.  By Theorem~\ref{thm:floor-pos-mono},
the floor itself cannot be removed; absolute catalysts merely close the
residual distance above floor in one step.
\end{remark}

\section{Locally-Impossible Catalysts}
\label{sec:ch14-locallyimpossible}

\begin{definition}[Locally-impossible catalyst]\label{def:loc-imp-mono}
A catalyst $\gamma \in \mathcal{E}$ is \emph{locally impossible} if
$S_{\mathcal{R}}(\gamma, x^{*}) = 100$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Locally-Impossible Catalysts Can Have Positive Power]
\label{thm:loc-imp-mono}
There exist locally-impossible catalysts $\gamma$ with
$\kappa_{\mathcal{R}, x^{*}}(\gamma) > 0$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Construct $\gamma := \eta + (-\eta + \delta)$ where $\eta$ is locally
maximally infeasible and $\delta$ is a small correction expression.  By
Theorem~\ref{thm:lg-decoupling-mono}, the local S-value of the first summand
$\eta$ is $100$, while the composite $\gamma$ has S-value equal to that of
$\delta$ alone, which we choose to be $S_{\flat}(\mathcal{R}) + \varepsilon$
for arbitrarily small $\varepsilon > 0$.  Substituting $\gamma$ into a parent
expression $\xi$ via $\diamond$ reduces the global S-distance by the amount
represented by $\delta$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The miracle principle, rigorously stated]
Theorem~\ref{thm:loc-imp-mono} formalises the notion that an expression need
not appear feasible at the local level to function as a catalyst at the global
level.  Locally infeasible (``miraculous'') subtasks compose into globally
feasible (``mathematical'') expressions.  This is the rigorous content of the
Miracle Principle: Corollary~\ref{cor:miracle-mono} shows that globally correct
expressions can have arbitrarily many locally maximally wrong subtasks.
\end{remark}

\section{The Catalyst Algebra}
\label{sec:ch14-algebra}

\begin{definition}[Catalyst algebra]\label{def:cat-algebra-mono}
The \emph{catalyst algebra} $\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{R}, x^{*}}$ of receiver
$\mathcal{R}$ at truth $x^{*}$ is the set
\[
  \mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{R}, x^{*}}
  := \{\gamma \in \mathcal{E} : \kappa_{\mathcal{R}, x^{*}}(\gamma) > 0\}
\]
equipped with the binary operation $\diamond$ of catalyst composition and the
unit $\mathbf{1}_{\diamond}$ being the empty expression (neutral catalyst).
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Monoid structure]\label{prop:monoid-mono}
$(\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{R}, x^{*}}, \diamond, \mathbf{1}_{\diamond})$ is a
monoid: the operation is associative and the unit is two-sided.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Associativity follows from the associativity of composition in $\mathcal{E}$
and the multiplicativity of catalytic power.  The unit is the empty expression,
whose application leaves the receiver unchanged.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Non-commutativity]\label{prop:non-comm-mono}
The catalyst algebra is in general non-commutative: there exist
$\gamma_1, \gamma_2 \in \mathcal{F}$ with
$\gamma_1 \diamond \gamma_2 \neq \gamma_2 \diamond \gamma_1$ as expressions,
even though their catalytic powers may coincide.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The order of catalyst application affects the intermediate state of the
receiver's knowledge, even when the final S-value is the same.
Theorem~\ref{thm:mult-cat-mono} gives the catalytic power of the composite as
a symmetric function of $\kappa_1, \kappa_2$, but the receiver-internal state
need not coincide under reordering.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Asymptotic Floor Approach]\label{thm:asymp-floor-mono}
For any sequence of catalysts $(\gamma_1, \gamma_2, \ldots)$ with
$\kappa_i > 0$:
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
  \item If $\sum_{i=1}^{\infty} \kappa_i = \infty$ (divergent sum), the cascade
    S-converges to floor.
  \item If $\sum_{i=1}^{\infty} \kappa_i < \infty$ (convergent sum), the cascade
    S-converges to a value strictly above floor.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By Corollary~\ref{cor:cascade-mono}, the cascade catalytic power after $n$
steps is $1 - \prod_{i=1}^{n}(1 - \kappa_i)$.  The infinite product converges
to $0$ (equivalently, the cascade power converges to $1$, driving S to floor)
if and only if $\sum \kappa_i = \infty$; otherwise the product converges to a
positive value, leaving a residual gap above floor.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Borel-Cantelli analogue]
Theorem~\ref{thm:asymp-floor-mono} is the catalytic analogue of the
Borel-Cantelli lemma: the divergence of the partial sums of catalytic powers
is the necessary and sufficient condition for almost-sure convergence to the
floor attractor.
\end{remark}

\section{Linear Justification Fails: Necessity of Circular Validity}
\label{sec:ch14-circular}

The Floor Theorem has a fundamental epistemological consequence: any
attempt to justify a foundational system by a linear chain of supporting
expressions is doomed to fail.

\begin{theorem}[Linear Justification Failure]\label{thm:linear-fail-mono}
For every receiver $\mathcal{R}$ with $S_{\flat}(\mathcal{R}) > 0$, there is
no finite sequence of expressions $\xi_0 \leftarrow \xi_1 \leftarrow \cdots
\leftarrow \xi_n$ such that:
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
  \item each $\xi_{i+1}$ supports $\xi_i$ to threshold $\theta = 1$;
  \item $\xi_n$ requires no support;
  \item $S_{\mathcal{R}}(\xi_0, x^{*}) = 0$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Condition~(iii) requires $S_{\mathcal{R}}(\xi_0, x^{*}) = 0$, but by
Theorem~\ref{thm:floor-pos-mono},
$S_{\mathcal{R}}(x, x^{*}) \geq S_{\flat}(\mathcal{R}) > 0$
for every candidate $x$.  Therefore no such sequence exists.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Infinite Regress Non-Termination]
A linear justification chain that does not terminate provides no foundation:
the chain has no element $\xi_n$ requiring no support, and the support of
$\xi_0$ is not established.
\end{corollary}

The only remaining option for foundational coherence is \emph{circular
validity}: a collection of at least three mutually supporting expressions with
a strongly connected validation graph.  We record this here as a structural
corollary of the Floor Theorem; the full theory of circular validation and
stability belongs to the broader calculus.

\section{Mass Spectrometric Realisation}
\label{sec:ch14-ms}

The abstract results of this chapter have immediate and precise realisations in
mass spectrometry.

\paragraph{The floor.}
For a mass spectrometric receiver $\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{MS}}$, the floor
$S_{\flat}(\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{MS}})$ is the smallest S-distance achievable
between a library match and the true molecular identity, given the instrument's
resolution, mass accuracy, and fragmentation coverage.  Theorem~\ref{thm:floor-pos-mono}
guarantees $S_{\flat} > 0$ regardless of how high the resolution or how
complete the library.

\paragraph{Information catalysts.}
A high-resolution MS/MS spectrum is an information catalyst: a single
fragmentation event simultaneously reduces $\Sk$ (structural distance), $\St$
(categorical distance), and $\Se$ (partition distance) for multiple candidate
molecules.  The catalytic power $\kappa$ of an MS/MS event is the fractional
reduction in S-distance it achieves; Theorem~\ref{thm:mult-cat-mono} governs
how multiple MS/MS events compose.

\paragraph{The unconstrained subtask.}
The multi-pass GPU architecture (Chapter~\ref{ch:gpu}) decomposes the global
spectral reconstruction task $\xi$ into shader passes $\eta_1, \ldots, \eta_n$.
Theorem~\ref{thm:unconstrained-mono} guarantees that each pass may be optimised
independently---its local S-value is irrelevant to the global correctness of
the final answer.  A shader pass that internally performs locally infeasible
intermediate computations (e.g., negative ion masses as intermediate variables)
is structurally admissible provided the global evaluation returns the correct
candidate.

\bigskip
\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=crownblue!5, colframe=crownblue!60!black,
                  title={\textbf{Chapter 14 Summary}}]
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
  \item \textbf{Floor Theorem}: for every bounded receiver $\mathcal{R}$,
    $S_{\flat}(\mathcal{R}) > 0$.  No candidate can have S-value below the
    floor; the floor is a receiver-intrinsic constant.
  \item \textbf{Corollary}: the smallest knowable error is
    $S_{\flat}(\mathcal{R})$, independent of the candidate or truth.
  \item \textbf{Unconstrained Subtask Theorem}: the global S-value of an
    expression does not constrain the local S-values of its subtasks.
    Locally maximally infeasible ($S=100$) subtasks can compose into a globally
    near-floor expression.
  \item \textbf{Local-Global Decoupling}: for any global S-value $S^{*}$ and
    any target local S-values $(\sigma_1,\ldots,\sigma_n)$, there exists a
    composed expression realising both simultaneously.
  \item \textbf{Catalytic power} is multiplicative under composition:
    $\kappa(\gamma_1 \diamond \gamma_2) = 1 - (1-\kappa_1)(1-\kappa_2)$.
  \item \textbf{Catalyst-driven convergence} is geometric: after $n$
    applications of a catalyst with power $\kappa$, the residual S-distance
    above floor is $(1-\kappa)^n$ times the initial residual.
  \item \textbf{Asymptotic floor approach}: a cascade of catalysts converges
    to floor if and only if the sum of catalytic powers diverges
    (Borel-Cantelli analogue).
  \item \textbf{Linear justification fails}: no linear chain of expressions
    attains $S=0$; foundational coherence requires circular validity with at
    least three mutually supporting expressions.
\end{itemize}
\end{tcolorbox}
