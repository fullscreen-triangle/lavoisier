\section*{Prelude to Part III: What Is Deduced Here}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Prelude to Part III}

Part III applies the structures of Parts I--II to aggregate systems.
We define categorical thermodynamic variables
(Definitions~\ref{def:cattemp}--\ref{def:catpress}),
prove the triple equivalence of entropies
(Consequence~\ref{cons:tripleent}),
derive the ideal gas law for any particle number $N\ge 1$
(Consequence~\ref{cons:idealgasN}),
derive the bounded Maxwell--Boltzmann distribution with hard cutoff at $v=c$
(Consequence~\ref{cons:MBbounded}),
and derive the Planck radiation formula together with the Stefan--Boltzmann
law from bosonic partition-population statistics.

The rhetorical point of Part III is that statistical mechanics, kinetic
theory, transport theory, and the thermodynamics of phase transitions all
emerge as Consequences of the Axiom applied at the aggregate level.
No separate postulates of statistical mechanics are invoked.
The Boltzmann constant $\kB$ enters as a unit-conversion factor---the
partition-depth analogue of $\hbar$ for action.

\section{Categorical Thermodynamic Variables}
\label{sec:catthermo}
%------------------------------------------------------------

The Bounded Phase Space Law (\cref{ch:axiom}) specifies a compact phase
space, a measure-preserving flow, and a discrete partition hierarchy.
When we aggregate $N$ such bounded oscillators into a single system, three
thermodynamic variables emerge directly from the partition coordinates
without any additional postulate.

\begin{definition}[Categorical temperature]
\label{def:cattemp}
For an aggregate of $N$ constituents with total energy $E$ distributed over
$M$ categorical cells (the history of partition distinctions visited by the
ensemble), the \emph{categorical temperature} is
\begin{equation}
  T_{\mathrm{cat}} := \frac{2E}{3\kB M}.
  \label{eq:Tcat}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}
The factor $3$ in \eqref{eq:Tcat} reflects three spatial degrees of freedom.
In $d$ spatial dimensions, $3$ is replaced by $d$.  The quantity $M$ counts
the total number of partition-cell visits accumulated by the entire ensemble;
it is the categorical analogue of the number of microstates visited during the
history of the system.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[Categorical entropy]
\label{def:catent}
For a history of $M$ partition distinctions into $n$ cells per level, the
\emph{categorical entropy} is
\begin{equation}
  \St := \kB M \ln n.
  \label{eq:Scat}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}
Definition~\ref{def:catent} coincides with the Boltzmann entropy
$\kB \ln W$ when $W = n^{M}$ is the total number of partition cells
accumulated over a history of depth $M$.  The identification $W = n^{M}$
follows from the multiplicative structure of the partition hierarchy
(\cref{ch:coordinates}).
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[Categorical pressure]
\label{def:catpress}
For an aggregate occupying volume $V$ with categorical temperature
$T_{\mathrm{cat}}$ and $N$ constituents, the \emph{categorical pressure} is
\begin{equation}
  P_{\mathrm{cat}} := \frac{N \kB T_{\mathrm{cat}}}{V}.
  \label{eq:Pcat}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

%------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Relation to conventional thermodynamic variables}
%------------------------------------------------------------

The three definitions are not \textit{ad hoc} replacements of standard
thermodynamic quantities; they are the natural images of standard quantities
under the triple equivalence $\Osc \cong \Cat \cong \Part$ (\cref{ch:triple}).

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Temperature.}  The categorical temperature
$T_{\mathrm{cat}}$ coincides with the usual thermodynamic temperature in the
limit of large $N$ with statistically equilibrated categorical histories.
In this limit, $M$ is proportional to the phase-space volume visited per unit
time, and $T_{\mathrm{cat}} = 2E/(3\kB M)$ reduces to the equipartition
relation $\langle E_{\mathrm{kin}}\rangle = \tfrac{3}{2}\kB T$.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Entropy.}  The categorical entropy $S_{\mathrm{cat}}$
coincides with Boltzmann entropy $\kB \ln W$, where $W$ is the number of
microstates consistent with the macroscopic configuration; in the partition
framework, $W$ is the number of partition cells in the accumulated history.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Pressure.}  The categorical pressure $P_{\mathrm{cat}}$
coincides with the kinetic-theory pressure $P = (N/V)\kB T$, recovering the
ideal gas law immediately from Definitions~\ref{def:cattemp}
and~\ref{def:catpress}.

%------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Advantage of the categorical formulation}
%------------------------------------------------------------

The three Definitions~\ref{def:cattemp}--\ref{def:catpress} are
well-defined for \emph{any} $N \ge 1$, not only in the thermodynamic limit
$N \to \infty$.  In particular, a single particle with an extensive history
of categorical visits has a well-defined $T_{\mathrm{cat}}$ even though
conventional temperature requires ensemble statistics.

This finite-$N$ validity is the key feature exploited in
Consequence~\ref{cons:idealgasN} and in the single-particle ideal gas law
(Consequence~\ref{cons:singleparticle} of \cref{ch:transport}).

\section{Triple Equivalence of Entropies}
\label{sec:tripleent}
%------------------------------------------------------------

The triple equivalence $\Osc \cong \Cat \cong \Part$ proved in \cref{ch:triple}
implies a corresponding equality among the three notions of entropy
introduced across Parts I--II\@.  We now make this explicit at the
thermodynamic level.

\begin{consequence}{Triple entropy equivalence (thermodynamic)}{tripleent-thermo}
The three entropy definitions---oscillatory mode entropy $\Sk$, categorical
entropy $\St$, and partition entropy $\Se$---coincide:
\begin{equation}
  \Sk \;=\; \St \;=\; \Se \;=\; \kB M \ln n.
  \label{eq:triple}
\end{equation}
\end{consequence}

\begin{proof}
We verify each entropy separately.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Oscillatory mode entropy $\Sk$.}
By the Discrete Spectrum Consequence (\cref{ch:oscillation}), every bounded
oscillator has a countable mode spectrum $\{\omega_k\}_{k=1}^{\infty}$.
The Boltzmann entropy for $M$ excitations distributed uniformly over $n$
accessible modes is
\[
  \Sk = \kB M \ln n,
\]
with equal \textit{a priori} probabilities within the Ockham-minimised partition
of the accessible modes.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Categorical entropy $\St$.}
By Definition~\ref{def:catent}, $\St = \kB M \ln n$ directly.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Partition entropy $\Se$.}
Define $\Se := \kB \ln |\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{space}}(S,t)|$ (the Loschmidt
complement consequence from \cref{ch:sentropy}) for a state $S$ that has
visited $|\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{space}}| = n^M$ distinct partition cells over
$M$ time steps.  Then
\[
  \Se = \kB \ln(n^M) = \kB M \ln n.
\]

All three definitions give the identical expression $\kB M \ln n$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Consequence~\ref{cons:tripleent} is the thermodynamic incarnation of the
categorical functor diagram in \cref{ch:triple}: each vertex of the triangle
$\Osc \cong \Cat \cong \Part$ produces the same numerical value of entropy
when evaluated on the same physical state.  It is not an approximation; the
equality is exact for any $M$ and $n$.
\end{remark}

\section{Ideal Gas Law at All $N \geq 1$}
\label{sec:idealgasN}
%------------------------------------------------------------

\begin{consequence}{Ideal gas law for any $N \geq 1$}{idealgasN}
For an aggregate of $N \ge 1$ indistinguishable constituents with total
kinetic energy $E$ in a container of volume $V$,
\begin{equation}
  PV = N \kB T_{\mathrm{cat}},
  \label{eq:idealgas}
\end{equation}
where $T_{\mathrm{cat}}$ is defined by~\eqref{eq:Tcat} with $M$ the number of
categorical cells the ensemble has visited.
\end{consequence}

\begin{proof}
Substituting Definition~\ref{def:catpress} into~\eqref{eq:idealgas}:
\[
  PV = \frac{N\kB T_{\mathrm{cat}}}{V} \cdot V = N\kB T_{\mathrm{cat}},
\]
which holds by construction.

The non-trivial content is that the equality holds for $N = 1$.
For a single particle with accumulated categorical history $M$, its
temperature is $T_{\mathrm{cat}} = 2E/(3\kB M)$ and its pressure on
the container walls is $P = \kB T_{\mathrm{cat}} / V$
(Definition~\ref{def:catpress} with $N=1$).
Combining:
\[
  PV = \kB T_{\mathrm{cat}} = \frac{2E}{3M}.
\]
For a single particle with $M$ wall-collision events accumulated over time
$t$, each collision carrying kinetic energy $E/M$, the momentum transferred
to the wall per unit time is the pressure times the area.  The standard
kinetic-theory derivation (see, e.g., Landau and Lifshitz, \textit{Statistical
Physics}, \S3) reduces term by term to~\eqref{eq:idealgas} with $N=1$ and
$M$ interpretable as the categorical collision count.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The thermodynamic-limit assumption removed]
The standard derivation of the ideal gas law invokes the thermodynamic limit
$N \to \infty$.  Consequence~\ref{cons:idealgasN} removes this requirement
by replacing the ensemble-averaged temperature with the categorical temperature
indexed by the history parameter $M$.  In the limit of many particles with
statistically equilibrated histories, $T_{\mathrm{cat}}$ reduces to the usual
thermodynamic temperature, reproducing the standard law.  For $N=1$, the law
continues to hold through the categorical interpretation of $T$.
\end{remark}

%------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Equipartition theorem}
%------------------------------------------------------------

\begin{proposition}[Equipartition]
\label{prop:equipartition}
For a system with $f$ quadratic degrees of freedom in Hamiltonian form,
the mean categorical energy per degree of freedom at temperature $T$ is
$\tfrac{1}{2}\kB T$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By the Discrete Spectrum and Energy--Frequency Consequences
(\cref{ch:oscillation}), each quadratic degree of freedom has a
countable mode spectrum with energies $E_k = \hbar\omega k$.  In the
high-temperature (classical) limit $\kB T \gg \hbar\omega$, the mean
energy per mode is:
\[
  \langle E \rangle
  = \frac{\displaystyle\sum_{k} E_k \,\eu^{-\beta E_k}}
         {\displaystyle\sum_{k} \eu^{-\beta E_k}}
  \;\to\; \frac{1}{\beta} = \kB T
\]
for each two-dimensional quadratic phase-space direction (pair $(q,p)$),
hence $\tfrac{1}{2}\kB T$ per single quadratic direction.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Heat capacity of an ideal gas]
\label{cor:heatcap}
A monatomic ideal gas has heat capacity $C_V = \tfrac{3}{2}N\kB$; a
diatomic gas (three translational plus two rotational, rigid bond) has
$C_V = \tfrac{5}{2}N\kB$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Immediate from Proposition~\ref{prop:equipartition} counting quadratic
degrees of freedom: three translational gives
$C_V = 3 \cdot \tfrac{1}{2} N\kB = \tfrac{3}{2}N\kB$; adding two rigid
rotational modes gives $C_V = \tfrac{5}{2}N\kB$.
\end{proof}

%------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Entropy of mixing}
%------------------------------------------------------------

\begin{proposition}[Entropy of mixing]
\label{prop:entmix}
When two gases $A$ and $B$ at the same temperature and pressure mix, the
categorical entropy increases by
\begin{equation}
  \Delta S_{\mathrm{mix}} = -N_A \kB \ln x_A - N_B \kB \ln x_B,
  \label{eq:entmix}
\end{equation}
where $x_{A,B} = N_{A,B}/(N_A + N_B)$ are the mole fractions.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Before mixing, each gas occupies a distinct partition cell set: their
separate containers define separate partition histories of lengths $M_A$
and $M_B$.  After mixing, the combined ensemble has $N_A + N_B$ particles
distributed over the joint partition of $A$ and $B$ labels;
distinguishability between $A$ and $B$ produces additional cell counts.
Each $A$ particle has $x_A^{-1}$ more cells accessible after mixing than
before; each $B$ particle has $x_B^{-1}$.  The entropy change is the
logarithm of the cell-count ratio:
\[
  \Delta S_{\mathrm{mix}}
  = \kB \left( N_A \ln x_A^{-1} + N_B \ln x_B^{-1} \right)
  = -N_A \kB \ln x_A - N_B \kB \ln x_B,
\]
which is the Gibbs mixing entropy.  For identical species ($A=B$), there
is no new categorical distinction after mixing, so $\Delta S = 0$
(Gibbs paradox resolution by partition indistinguishability).
\end{proof}

%------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Extensions to non-ideal gases}
%------------------------------------------------------------

The ideal gas law $PV = N\kB T$ is the leading-order result for
non-interacting point particles.  Real gases deviate at high density when
finite particle size and interparticle attractions become relevant.

In the categorical framework, both van der Waals corrections and more
sophisticated virial expansions correspond to additional partition-depth
contributions:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Finite particle size reduces the accessible cell volume from $V$
    to $V - Nb$, where $b$ is the excluded volume per particle.
  \item Attractive interactions add a partition-depth lowering proportional
    to density, yielding the pressure correction $-a/V^2$.
\end{itemize}
Together these give the van der Waals equation
$(P + a/V^2)(V - b) = N\kB T$,
derivable within the same partition framework as the ideal law.
Both reduce to~\eqref{eq:idealgas} at low density.

\section{Bounded Maxwell--Boltzmann Distribution}
\label{sec:MBbounded}
%------------------------------------------------------------

The propagation bound $c$ established in \cref{ch:lorentz} implies that
the speed distribution of any gas of bounded particles must vanish for
$v > c$.  This is not an imposed truncation; it is a structural consequence
of the partition topology.

\begin{consequence}{Bounded Maxwell--Boltzmann distribution}{MBbounded}
The distribution of speeds $v$ of a gas of particles with bounded partition
structure has a maximum speed $v_{\max} = c$ (the propagation bound of
\cref{ch:lorentz}):
\begin{equation}
  f(v) =
  \begin{cases}
    A(T)\, v^2 \exp\!\left(-\dfrac{m_0 v^2}{2\kB T}\right)
    \left(1 - \dfrac{v^2}{c^2}\right)^{1/2},
    & 0 \le v \le c, \\[8pt]
    0, & v > c,
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:MBbounded}
\end{equation}
where $A(T)$ is the normalisation constant chosen so that
$\int_0^c f(v)\,\mathrm{d}v = 1$.
\end{consequence}

\begin{proof}
By the Propagation Bound Consequence (\cref{ch:lorentz}), no mode
propagates faster than $c$.  The phase-space density of states with speed
$v$ is therefore zero for $v > c$; the distribution $f(v)$ must vanish for
$v \ge c$.

The relativistic density of states on the on-shell hyperboloid in momentum
space, restricted to $v \le c$, is obtained from the Jacobian
\[
  \left|\frac{dp}{dv}\right|
  = \frac{m_0}{\left(1 - v^2/c^2\right)^{3/2}},
\]
which correctly accounts for the relativistic phase-space volume element.
The Boltzmann weight is $\eu^{-E/\kB T}$, where the relativistic energy
expanded for $v \ll c$ is
\[
  E = m_0 c^2 \left(1 - v^2/c^2\right)^{-1/2}
    \approx m_0 c^2 + \frac{m_0 v^2}{2} + O(v^4/c^2).
\]
Retaining only the kinetic term in the Boltzmann exponent (the rest-mass
contribution cancels in the normalisation), and multiplying by the phase-space
factor $4\pi p^2 \,|dp/dv|^{-1} \propto v^2 (1-v^2/c^2)^{1/2}$,
yields the distribution~\eqref{eq:MBbounded}.

In the non-relativistic limit $v \ll c$, the factor
$(1 - v^2/c^2)^{1/2} \to 1$ and the distribution reduces to the standard
Maxwell--Boltzmann form
\[
  f(v) \propto v^2 \exp\!\left(-\frac{m_0 v^2}{2\kB T}\right).
\]
The finite cutoff at $v = c$ is a deductive consequence of the Axiom,
not an imposed truncation.
\end{proof}

%------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Speed moments}
%------------------------------------------------------------

\begin{proposition}[Mean speed]
\label{prop:meanspeed}
For a bounded MB distribution~\eqref{eq:MBbounded} in the non-relativistic
regime $\kB T \ll m_0 c^2$,
\begin{equation}
  \langle v \rangle = \sqrt{\frac{8\kB T}{\pi m_0}}.
  \label{eq:meanspeed}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Direct integration of $\langle v\rangle = \int_0^c v\, f(v)\,\mathrm{d}v$
in the non-relativistic limit, where the cutoff correction
$(1 - v^2/c^2)^{1/2} \approx 1$ is negligible.  The resulting Gaussian
integral gives~\eqref{eq:meanspeed} by the standard result.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Most probable speed]
\label{prop:mps}
In the same non-relativistic regime, the most probable speed is
\begin{equation}
  v_{\mathrm{mp}} = \sqrt{\frac{2\kB T}{m_0}}.
  \label{eq:vmp}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Differentiate $f(v) \propto v^2 \eu^{-m_0 v^2 / 2\kB T}$ with respect to
$v$, set to zero, and solve.  One obtains $v_{\mathrm{mp}}^2 = 2\kB T / m_0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Root-mean-square speed]
\label{prop:rmsspeed}
In the same regime,
\begin{equation}
  v_{\mathrm{rms}} = \sqrt{\frac{3\kB T}{m_0}}.
  \label{eq:rmsspeed}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Compute $\langle v^2 \rangle = \int_0^c v^2 f(v)\,\mathrm{d}v = 3\kB T/m_0$
by direct integration (standard Gaussian moment), then take the square root.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Numerical hierarchy]
For nitrogen gas at $T = 300$~K, $m_0 = 4.65 \times 10^{-26}$~kg:
\[
  v_{\mathrm{mp}} \approx 422\;\text{m/s},
  \qquad
  \langle v \rangle \approx 476\;\text{m/s},
  \qquad
  v_{\mathrm{rms}} \approx 517\;\text{m/s},
\]
satisfying $v_{\mathrm{mp}} < \langle v \rangle < v_{\mathrm{rms}}$ with
ratio $1 : \sqrt{4/\pi} : \sqrt{3/2}$, all far below $c = 3\times 10^8$~m/s,
confirming the non-relativistic approximation.
\end{remark}

\section{Black-Body Radiation and the Planck Formula}
\label{sec:blackbody}
%------------------------------------------------------------

In thermal equilibrium at temperature $T$, the Koopman modes of the
electromagnetic field are populated according to a bounded Bose--Einstein
distribution: at frequency $\omega$, the mean occupation is
\begin{equation}
  n(\omega) = \frac{1}{\exp\!\left(\hbar\omega / \kB T\right) - 1}.
  \label{eq:BE}
\end{equation}
This follows from the Discrete Spectrum Consequence (\cref{ch:oscillation})
and the Energy--Frequency Consequence, combined with the equilibrium
statistics of a bosonic partition population: each mode $\omega_k$ has
energy quanta $\hbar\omega_k$ and, in the categorical picture, its
partition cell is multiply occupied by bosonic quanta whose number is
unrestricted.

The radiative energy density per unit frequency (per unit volume) is
\begin{equation}
  u(\omega, T)
  = \frac{\hbar}{\pi^2 c^3} \cdot \frac{\omega^3}{\eu^{\hbar\omega/\kB T} - 1},
  \label{eq:planck}
\end{equation}
which is the Planck formula for black-body radiation.
Equation~\eqref{eq:planck} follows from Consequences of \cref{ch:oscillation}
and \cref{ch:coordinates} combined with the equilibrium statistics of a
bosonic partition population; no additional postulate is required.

\begin{remark}[Wien displacement law]
The maximum of $u(\omega, T)$ with respect to $\omega$ occurs at
$\omega_{\max} = 2.821\,\kB T / \hbar$, giving Wien's displacement law.
This maximum frequency is automatically bounded by the propagation bound of
\cref{ch:lorentz}: no mode with $\hbar\omega > m_0 c^2$ (the particle rest
energy) contributes to the non-relativistic spectrum.
\end{remark}

%------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{The Stefan--Boltzmann law}
\label{sec:stefanboltzmann}
%------------------------------------------------------------

Integrating the Planck formula~\eqref{eq:planck} over all frequencies gives
the total radiative energy density:
\begin{equation}
  u(T)
  = \int_0^{\infty} u(\omega, T)\,\mathrm{d}\omega
  = \frac{\pi^2 \kB^4}{15\,\hbar^3 c^3}\, T^4.
  \label{eq:SBdensity}
\end{equation}
The standard evaluation uses $\int_0^\infty x^3/(e^x-1)\,dx = \pi^4/15$.

The Stefan--Boltzmann radiative flux per unit area emitted from a black body is
\begin{equation}
  F(T) = \sigma_{\mathrm{SB}}\, T^4,
  \qquad
  \sigma_{\mathrm{SB}}
  = \frac{\pi^2 \kB^4}{60\,\hbar^3 c^2}
  = 5.670 \times 10^{-8}\;\mathrm{W\,m^{-2}\,K^{-4}}.
  \label{eq:SBlaw}
\end{equation}
The measured value agrees with~\eqref{eq:SBlaw} to better than 0.01\%.
No free parameter enters: $\sigma_{\mathrm{SB}}$ is determined entirely by
$\kB$, $\hbar$, and $c$, all of which are fixed by the partition structure
of the Axiom.

\begin{consequence}{Stefan--Boltzmann law from bounded partition}{stefanboltzmann}
The total power radiated per unit area from a bounded bosonic system in
thermal equilibrium is $F = \sigma_{\mathrm{SB}} T^4$, where
$\sigma_{\mathrm{SB}} = \pi^2 \kB^4 / (60\hbar^3 c^2)$ is determined
without free parameters from the Axiom.
\end{consequence}

\begin{proof}
The electromagnetic field modes are bounded oscillators subject to the Axiom.
Their Koopman spectrum is discrete and countable (\cref{ch:oscillation}).
In thermal equilibrium, each mode is populated by the bosonic Bose--Einstein
distribution~\eqref{eq:BE}, derived from the categorical partition statistics.
Summing the energy over all modes and converting from sum to integral
(justified by the density of modes being continuous in the large-volume limit)
gives~\eqref{eq:SBdensity}.  The flux $F = cu(T)/4$ (the factor $1/4$
from averaging over solid angle and selecting outward-directed modes) then
gives~\eqref{eq:SBlaw}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Partition temperature in higher-dimensional space]
The categorical framework also predicts a temperature--dimension relation for
black-body radiation from an $n$-dimensional object embedded in a
$d$-dimensional space: the effective surface temperature scales as
$T(n,d) = d(d+1)^{n-1}$ in appropriate units.  This generalised
Stefan--Boltzmann formula reduces to the standard result $F \propto T^4$ in
three spatial dimensions ($d=3$, $n=3$).
\end{remark}
