\section{Motivation: Why a New Entropy?}

Shannon entropy $H$ and thermodynamic entropy $S$ both measure uncertainty,
but neither is indexed to a specific receiver's epistemic state.
Shannon entropy is defined relative to a source distribution; thermodynamic
entropy is defined relative to a macrostate.
Neither gives the distance between a receiver's current knowledge and the
truth about a specific question.

The S-entropy calculus supplies this missing piece.
It is not a generalisation of Shannon or Boltzmann entropy; it is a distinct
axiomatic system that measures \emph{distance to truth} on a bounded scale,
relative to a specified receiver.

\section{The S-Scale}
\label{sec:ch13-scale}

\begin{definition}[S-scale]
  The S-scale is the bounded interval $[0, 100] \subset \mathbb{R}$, where
  $S = 100$ represents complete ignorance (maximum distance from truth) and
  $S = 0$ would represent perfect knowledge.
  The scale is receiver-relative: $S$ is defined with respect to a specified
  receiver $R$.
\end{definition}

The bounds are not arbitrary.
$S = 100$ corresponds to the maximum entropy state of the receiver's bounded
information capacity (a consequence of BPSL applied to receivers).
$S = 0$ is the limit of perfect knowledge, which the Floor Theorem
(Chapter~\ref{ch:floor}) shows no bounded receiver can attain.

\section{The Receiver Quadruple}
\label{sec:ch13-receiver}

\begin{definition}[Receiver]
  A \emph{receiver} is a quadruple $R = (\Sigma_R, \Phi_R, K_R, \mathrm{dec}_R)$
  where:
  \begin{itemize}
    \item $\Sigma_R$ is the receiver's state space (a bounded partition space
      satisfying BPSL);
    \item $\Phi_R : \Sigma_R \to \Sigma_R$ is the receiver's update dynamics
      (measure-preserving);
    \item $K_R \subset \Sigma_R$ is the receiver's current knowledge state;
    \item $\mathrm{dec}_R : \Sigma_R \to \{0,1\}^*$ is the receiver's
      decoding function (the map from internal state to expressed beliefs).
  \end{itemize}
\end{definition}

A mass spectrometer is a receiver: $\Sigma_R$ is the partition space of
measurable $m/z$ values; $\Phi_R$ is the update from one scan to the next;
$K_R$ is the current spectral library; $\mathrm{dec}_R$ maps detected peaks
to molecular formula assignments.

\section{The S-Functional: Four Axioms}
\label{sec:ch13-functional}

\begin{definition}[S-functional]
  The \emph{S-functional} $\mathbf{S}[R, q]$ is the S-value assigned by
  receiver $R$ to question $q$.
  It satisfies four axioms:
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Boundedness.} $0 \leq \mathbf{S}[R,q] \leq 100$ for all $R$
      and $q$.
    \item \textbf{Monotone informativeness.} If observation $o$ at receiver $R$
      increases the probability of the correct answer to $q$, then
      $\mathbf{S}[R \oplus o, q] \leq \mathbf{S}[R, q]$.
    \item \textbf{Additivity under independence.} If $q_1$ and $q_2$ are
      independent questions, $\mathbf{S}[R, q_1 \wedge q_2] = \mathbf{S}[R,q_1]
      + \mathbf{S}[R,q_2] - \mathbf{S}[R,q_1]\mathbf{S}[R,q_2]/100$.
    \item \textbf{Continuity.} $\mathbf{S}[R, q]$ varies continuously in the
      receiver state $K_R$ under the topology induced by the partition algebra.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\section{The S-Expression Triple}
\label{sec:ch13-triple}

In mass spectrometric applications, the relevant questions are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $\Sk$: S-value for the question ``what is the molecular structure?''
    (knowledge from spectral matching)
  \item $\St$: S-value for the question ``at what time does this ion arrive?''
    (temporal resolution of the instrument)
  \item $\Se$: S-value for the question ``which partition cell does this ion
    occupy?''  (entropy of the partition at the instrument's resolution depth)
\end{itemize}

The triple $(\Sk, \St, \Se)$ is the S-expression tuple instantiated for
mass spectrometric observables.
Each component is bounded in $[0, 100]$; their joint variation maps the
instrument's epistemic state.
