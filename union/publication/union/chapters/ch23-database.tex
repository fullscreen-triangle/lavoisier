\section{The Storage Paradox}
\label{sec:ch23-paradox}

Traditional mass-spectrometric identification rests on a correspondence
assumption: a measured spectrum corresponds to an entry in a stored library,
and identification succeeds when a similarity score between the two exceeds a
tuned threshold.  PubChem catalogues $N \approx 10^8$ unique chemical
structures.  Each structure potentially yields spectral data across multiple
analytical dimensions: fragmentation patterns, isotope distributions, retention
characteristics, collision cross-sections.  A single query incurs
$O(N \times d)$ comparisons, where $d$ is the spectral dimensionality.  At
the scale of all synthesisable drug-like molecules ($\sim 10^{60}$ by
combinatorial estimates), the correspondence paradigm collapses entirely: one
cannot store spectra for compounds that have never been synthesised, let alone
measured.

This situation prompts a foundational question that the bounded-phase-space
framework is equipped to answer: \emph{does a spectrum need to exist before it
is looked up?}  If the physics governing ion behaviour in a mass analyser is
deterministic---and it is, governed by classical electrodynamics at the
relevant energy scales---then the spectrum of any compound is a consequence of
that compound's physical properties.  A structure that encodes the physics can
\emph{generate} any entry rather than store pre-measured copies.

The present chapter constructs that structure.  Connecting the mathematical
foundations of Chapters~\ref{ch:axiom}--\ref{ch:sentropy} with the Lagrangian
apparatus of Chapter~\ref{ch:lagrangian}, we arrive at a database with a
paradoxical property: it contains nothing, yet can answer any query.

\section{S-Entropy Coordinate Space}
\label{sec:ch23-sentropy}

\subsection{Three Dimensions of Molecular Identity}
\label{sec:ch23-three-dims}

Chapter~\ref{ch:sentropy} established the S-entropy triple $(\Sk, \St, \Se)$
as a compact encoding of molecular oscillatory identity.  We restate the
precise definitions here, collecting all numerical data needed for the
generative database.

\begin{definition}[Knowledge Entropy $\Sk$]
\label{def:ch23-sk}
For a polyatomic molecule ($\Nvib \geq 2$) with vibrational frequencies
$\{\omega_i\}_{i=1}^{\Nvib}$:
\begin{enumerate}[(a)]
  \item Compute the normalised frequency distribution:
    $p_i = \omega_i \big/ \sum_{j=1}^{\Nvib} \omega_j$.
  \item Compute the Shannon entropy:
    $H = -\sum_{i=1}^{\Nvib} p_i \log_2 p_i$.
  \item Normalise: $\Sk = H \big/ \log_2 \Nvib$.
\end{enumerate}
For a diatomic molecule ($\Nvib = 1$) with single vibrational frequency
$\omega$:
\[
  \Sk = \omega / \omega_{\mathrm{ref}},
\]
where $\omega_{\mathrm{ref}} = 4401\;\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ is the H$_2$
fundamental frequency (highest known diatomic).
\end{definition}

The knowledge entropy measures how uniformly vibrational energy is distributed
across modes.  When all modes have equal frequency, $\Sk = 1$ (maximum
uniformity).  When one mode dominates, $\Sk \to 0$.  For water
($\{1595, 3657, 3756\}\;\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$): $\Sk = 0.944$.  For HCl
($2886\;\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$): $\Sk = 0.656$.

\begin{definition}[Temporal Entropy $\St$]
\label{def:ch23-st}
For a polyatomic molecule ($\Nvib \geq 2$):
\[
  \St = \frac{\log(\omega_{\max}/\omega_{\min})}
             {\log(\omega_{\mathrm{ref,max}}/\omega_{\mathrm{ref,min}})},
\]
where $\omega_{\mathrm{ref,max}} = 4401\;\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ (H$_2$ stretch)
and $\omega_{\mathrm{ref,min}} = 67\;\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ (I$_2$ stretch)
define the reference dynamic range.

For a diatomic molecule ($\Nvib = 1$) with rotational constant $\Brot$:
\[
  \St = \frac{\log(\omega/\Brot)}
             {\log(\omega_{\mathrm{ref,max}}/\omega_{\mathrm{ref,min}})}.
\]
\end{definition}

The temporal entropy measures the dynamic range of the vibrational spectrum:
the ratio between the fastest and slowest oscillatory modes.  For CO$_2$:
$\St = 0.301$.  For N$_2$ (diatomic, $\omega = 2330\;\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$,
$\Brot = 1.998\;\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$): $\St = 0.733$.

\begin{definition}[Harmonic Proximity]
\label{def:ch23-harmonic}
Two frequencies $\omega_a$ and $\omega_b$ are in \emph{harmonic proximity}
if there exist integers $p, q$ with $1 \leq p, q \leq 8$ such that
\[
  \left|\frac{\max(\omega_a,\omega_b)}{\min(\omega_a,\omega_b)} -
        \frac{p}{q}\right| < \delta,
\]
where $\delta = 0.05$ is the harmonic tolerance.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Evolution Entropy $\Se$]
\label{def:ch23-se}
For a molecule with $\Nvib \geq 2$:
\[
  \Se = \frac{N_{\mathrm{harmonic}}}{\max(N_{\mathrm{pairs}},1)},
\]
where $N_{\mathrm{pairs}} = \binom{\Nvib}{2}$ is the total number of
frequency pairs and $N_{\mathrm{harmonic}}$ is the number of pairs in
harmonic proximity.  For diatomic molecules ($\Nvib = 1$): $\Se = 0$.
\end{definition}

The evolution entropy measures the degree of harmonic (integer-ratio)
relationships among modes.  High $\Se$ indicates strong mode coupling through
Fermi resonance; low $\Se$ indicates dynamically independent modes.  For
water, all three pairs are harmonic: $\Se = 1.0$.

\subsection{The S-Entropy Space}
\label{sec:ch23-space}

\begin{definition}[S-Entropy Space]
\label{def:ch23-space}
The \emph{S-entropy space} is the unit cube
\[
  \mathcal{S} = [0,1]^3 = \{(\Sk, \St, \Se) :
    0 \leq \Sk, \St, \Se \leq 1\},
\]
equipped with the Euclidean metric $d(\mathbf{s}_1, \mathbf{s}_2) =
\|\mathbf{s}_1 - \mathbf{s}_2\|_2$.
\end{definition}

The space $\mathcal{S}$ is compact (closed and bounded in $\mathbb{R}^3$,
hence compact by Heine--Borel), complete, and supports a well-defined
Euclidean metric.  Every stable molecule maps to a unique point in
$\mathcal{S}$.

\begin{theorem}[Unique Resolution]
\label{thm:ch23-unique-resolution}
Distinct molecules with distinct vibrational frequency spectra have distinct
S-entropy coordinates in $\mathcal{S}$.  That is, the map
$\Phi : \mathcal{M} \to [0,1]^3$ sending each molecular species to its
S-entropy triple is injective on the space $\mathcal{M}$ of molecular
species at any fixed finite resolution.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The kinetic coordinate $\Sk$ encodes the normalised energy distribution across
modes, which encodes the relative frequency structure.  The temporal coordinate
$\St$ encodes the dynamic range $\omega_{\max}/\omega_{\min}$, which encodes
the overall scale separation.  The evolution coordinate $\Se$ encodes the
harmonic coupling network, which encodes the topological pattern of inter-mode
rational relationships.

Two molecules with identical $(\Sk, \St, \Se)$ would have identical energy
distributions, identical dynamic ranges, and identical harmonic coupling
networks.  By the Oscillatory Necessity theorem (Chapter~\ref{ch:oscillation}),
such molecules have identical frequency spectra $\{\omega_i\}$.  Identical
frequency spectra imply identical force-constant matrices, hence identical
bonding topology and atomic mass distribution, hence chemical identity.
Therefore $\Phi$ is injective.
\end{proof}

\begin{consequence}{S-Entropy Coordinates as Molecular Identity}{ch23-identity}
The triple $(\Sk, \St, \Se) \in [0,1]^3$ is a complete intrinsic
identifier for any stable molecular species at spectroscopic resolution.  Two
molecules that agree on all three coordinates are the same molecule.  The
S-entropy space is the natural phase space for the generative database.
\end{consequence}

\section{Ternary Encoding}
\label{sec:ch23-ternary}

\subsection{Why Base 3}
\label{sec:ch23-base3}

The S-entropy space $\mathcal{S} = [0,1]^3$ is three-dimensional.  A natural
question is what numerical base is optimal for hierarchically encoding a point
in this three-dimensional space.

\begin{theorem}[Ternary Naturalness]
\label{thm:ch23-ternary}
Among all integer-base hierarchical encodings of $[0,1]^3$, base~3 minimises
the radix economy $J(b) = b / \ln b$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The radix economy measures encoding cost: $J(b) = b/\ln b$ counts
(number of digits) $\times$ (symbols per digit), normalised by information
per digit.  Setting $J'(b) = (\ln b - 1)/(\ln b)^2 = 0$ gives $b = e \approx
2.718$.  The nearest integers give $J(2) = 2/\ln 2 \approx 2.885$ and
$J(3) = 3/\ln 3 \approx 2.731$.  Since $J(3) < J(2) < J(4)$, base~3 is
optimal among integers.

Moreover, for $d = 3$ dimensions, base $b = 3$ achieves an exact match:
each trit (ternary digit) refines exactly one coordinate into three equal
subintervals, and one complete cycle of three trits refines all three
dimensions by exactly one level, partitioning $[0,1]^3$ into $27$ congruent
subcubes.  No other integer base achieves this perfect dimensional alignment.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The Interleaving Scheme}
\label{sec:ch23-interleave}

\begin{definition}[Interleaved Ternary Address]
\label{def:ch23-address}
Given S-entropy coordinates $(\Sk, \St, \Se) \in [0,1]^3$, the
\emph{interleaved ternary address} of depth $k$ is the string $a_1 a_2
\cdots a_k$ where each $a_j \in \{0, 1, 2\}$ is computed by successive
trisection: at step $j$, dimension $d = (j-1) \bmod 3$ (with $0 \leftrightarrow
\Sk$, $1 \leftrightarrow \St$, $2 \leftrightarrow \Se$) is partitioned into
three equal subintervals, and $a_j$ records which subinterval contains the
coordinate value.
\end{definition}

The interleaving ensures balanced resolution: after $3m$ steps, every
dimension has been refined $m$ times.  Position $j$ with $j \equiv 1 \pmod 3$
refines $\Sk$; $j \equiv 2 \pmod 3$ refines $\St$; $j \equiv 0 \pmod 3$
refines $\Se$.

\begin{example}[Water]
For \ce{H2O} with $(\Sk, \St, \Se) = (0.944, 0.419, 1.000)$:
Trit~1 ($\Sk$): $0.944 \in [2/3,1]$, so $a_1 = 2$.
Trit~2 ($\St$): $0.419 \in [1/3,2/3]$, so $a_2 = 1$.
Trit~3 ($\Se$): $1.000 \in [2/3,1]$, so $a_3 = 2$.
Continuing to depth~12: the full address is \texttt{212221112122}.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[HCl]
For HCl with $(\Sk, \St, \Se) = (0.656, 0.757, 0.000)$:
Trit~1 ($\Sk$): $0.656 \in [1/3,2/3]$, so $a_1 = 1$.
Trit~2 ($\St$): $0.757 \in [2/3,1]$, so $a_2 = 2$.
Trit~3 ($\Se$): $0.000 \in [0,1/3]$, so $a_3 = 0$.
Full 12-trit address: \texttt{120120210200}.
\end{example}

\subsection{Trit--Cell Bijection}
\label{sec:ch23-bijection-trit}

\begin{theorem}[Trit--Cell Bijection]
\label{thm:ch23-trit-cell}
There exists a bijection between the set of $k$-trit strings $\{0,1,2\}^k$
and the set of level-$k$ cells in the ternary partition of $[0,1]^3$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
We proceed by induction.  Base case ($k = 0$): the empty string corresponds
to the single cell $[0,1]^3$.  Inductive step: given the bijection at depth
$k$, the $(k+1)$-th trit refines dimension $d = k \bmod 3$ of each level-$k$
cell into three equal sub-intervals.  The trit value $a_{k+1} \in \{0,1,2\}$
selects one of these three subcells.  The map $a_1 \cdots a_k a_{k+1} \mapsto$
(the $a_{k+1}$-th subcell of the cell corresponding to $a_1 \cdots a_k$) is
injective (different strings map to different cells) and surjective (every
level-$(k+1)$ cell arises from exactly one level-$k$ cell and one trit value).
The bijection holds for all $k$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Distance Preservation}
\label{sec:ch23-dist}

\begin{theorem}[Prefix Distance Bound]
\label{thm:ch23-prefix-distance}
Two ternary strings sharing a common prefix of length $k$ address cells
whose centres lie within Euclidean distance at most
$\sqrt{3} \cdot 3^{-\lfloor k/3 \rfloor}$ of each other.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
After $k$ trits, each dimension $d$ has been refined $\lfloor (k - d)/3
\rfloor + [d < k \bmod 3]$ times, so its coordinate interval has width at
most $3^{-\lfloor k/3 \rfloor}$.  Two points sharing a $k$-trit prefix
lie in the same level-$k$ cell.  The maximum distance within a cell with
side lengths $\Delta_1, \Delta_2, \Delta_3 \leq 3^{-\lfloor k/3 \rfloor}$
is the space diagonal $\sqrt{\Delta_1^2 + \Delta_2^2 + \Delta_3^2} \leq
\sqrt{3} \cdot 3^{-\lfloor k/3 \rfloor}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Position--Trajectory Duality]
\label{thm:ch23-duality}
Every ternary string $\sigma = a_1 a_2 \cdots a_k$ simultaneously encodes two
mathematical objects: (a) the cell $C(\sigma) \subset [0,1]^3$ it specifies
(a position in S-entropy space), and (b) the sequence of refinement choices
that produced that cell (a trajectory of measurement decisions in the ternary
trie $\mathcal{T}$).  These are the same mathematical object: the address
\emph{is} the path.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The cell $C(\sigma)$ is uniquely determined by $\sigma$ (Theorem
\ref{thm:ch23-trit-cell}).  The path $v_0 \to v_1 \to \cdots \to v_k$ through
the trie is also uniquely determined by $\sigma$: each trit $a_j$ selects a
unique child node.  Since the bijection is invertible, specifying
\emph{where} a molecule is in S-entropy space (position) is the same as
specifying \emph{how} it was classified (trajectory through the measurement
hierarchy).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
This duality dissolves the classical separation between the observed system and
the measurement apparatus.  In the ternary encoding, the act of observation
(traversing the trie) and the state observed (the cell reached) are the same
mathematical object.  The address \emph{is} the identity \emph{is} the
measurement record.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Continuous Emergence}
\label{sec:ch23-emergence}

\begin{theorem}[Continuous Emergence]
\label{thm:ch23-convergence}
As $k \to \infty$, the ternary expansion converges to a unique point
in $[0,1]^3$.  Every point in $[0,1]^3$ is the limit of exactly one infinite
ternary expansion (up to the convention for terminating expansions).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For each $k$, the cell $C_k = C(a_1 \cdots a_k)$ is a closed rectangular
box with $C_{k+1} \subset C_k$.  The sequence $\{C_k\}_{k=0}^\infty$ is
nested, non-empty, and closed.  By Theorem~\ref{thm:ch23-prefix-distance},
$\mathrm{diam}(C_k) \leq \sqrt{3} \cdot 3^{-\lfloor k/3 \rfloor} \to 0$.
By Cantor's intersection theorem, $\bigcap_{k=0}^\infty C_k$ is non-empty.
Since the diameter tends to zero, the intersection contains exactly one point
$(\Sk, \St, \Se)$.
\end{proof}

\section{The Partition Lagrangian}
\label{sec:ch23-lagrangian}

\subsection{Partition Geometry and Charge Emergence}
\label{sec:ch23-geometry}

The internal structure of each cell in the ternary trie corresponds to a
hierarchical partition of the molecule's oscillatory phase space.
Chapter~\ref{ch:coordinates} established the partition coordinates
$(n, l, m, s)$ with shell capacity $C(n) = 2n^2$.  Within this geometry,
electric charge emerges as a partition property: a molecular ion is a molecule
with an incomplete categorical structure, and $m/z$ measures inertia per unit
partition asymmetry.

\begin{definition}[Partition Inertia]
\label{def:ch23-inertia}
The \emph{partition inertia} of an ion with mass $m$ and charge state $z$ is
$\partinert = \alpha(m/z)$, where $\alpha$ is a coupling constant with
dimensions of energy $\cdot$ time$^2$ $\cdot$ length$^{-2}$.
\end{definition}

\subsection{The Lagrangian Construction}
\label{sec:ch23-construction}

\begin{theorem}[Partition Lagrangian]
\label{thm:ch23-lagrangian}
The Lagrangian governing the trajectory of an ion with partition inertia
$\partinert$ in a partition depth field $\Depth(\mathbf{x}, t)$ and partition
vector potential $\Apartition(\mathbf{x})$ is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ch23-lagrangian}
  \Lagr_\Depth = \frac{1}{2}\partinert|\dot{\mathbf{x}}|^2
               + \partinert\,\dot{\mathbf{x}} \cdot \Apartition(\mathbf{x})
               - \Depth(\mathbf{x}, t).
\end{equation}
This Lagrangian is derived from three requirements: Galilean invariance of
the kinetic term, scalar coupling to the depth field, and vector coupling to
the partition vector potential.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Requirement~1 (Galilean invariance):} For a particle with effective
inertia $\partinert$, the kinetic energy must be a Galilean scalar:
$T = \tfrac{1}{2}\partinert|\dot{\mathbf{x}}|^2$.

\emph{Requirement~2 (Scalar coupling):} The partition depth $\Depth(\mathbf{x}, t)$
is a scalar field---at each point in space it assigns a number measuring the
refinement depth of the local partition structure.  The simplest
Galilean-invariant coupling to a scalar field is potential energy:
$V = \Depth(\mathbf{x}, t)$, entering with a minus sign by convention
$\Lagr = T - V$.

\emph{Requirement~3 (Vector coupling):} When the partition device involves
magnetic fields or rotating reference frames (as in FT-ICR and quadrupole
analysers), a vector potential $\Apartition(\mathbf{x})$ couples to velocity.
The unique Galilean-covariant coupling is
$\partinert\dot{\mathbf{x}} \cdot \Apartition(\mathbf{x})$, analogous to
electromagnetic minimal coupling.

Combining the three terms yields \eqref{eq:ch23-lagrangian}.  The
Euler--Lagrange equations $\tfrac{d}{dt}\tfrac{\partial\Lagr}{\partial
\dot{x}_i} = \tfrac{\partial\Lagr}{\partial x_i}$ recover
$\partinert\ddot{\mathbf{x}} = -\nabla\Depth +
\partinert\dot{\mathbf{x}} \times \mathbf{B}_\Depth$ where
$\mathbf{B}_\Depth = \nabla \times \Apartition$, the partition curl field.
This has the same mathematical structure as the Lorentz force law.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Correspondence with electromagnetism]
The partition Lagrangian is structurally identical to the electromagnetic
Lagrangian $\Lagr_{\mathrm{EM}} = \frac{1}{2}m|\dot{\mathbf{x}}|^2 +
q\dot{\mathbf{x}} \cdot \mathbf{A} - q\phi$, with the identification
$\partinert \leftrightarrow m$, $\Apartition \leftrightarrow \mathbf{A}$,
$\Depth \leftrightarrow q\phi$.  The electromagnetic fields in a mass analyser
\emph{are} partition fields: they implement the physical mechanism by which the
analyser classifies ions.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The Fundamental Identity}
\label{sec:ch23-fundamental}

\begin{theorem}[Fundamental Identity]
\label{thm:ch23-fundamental}
The rate of partition depth change equals the oscillation frequency divided
by $2\pi$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ch23-fundamental}
  \frac{d\Depth}{dt} = \frac{\omega}{2\pi} = \frac{1}{\langle\taup\rangle},
\end{equation}
where $\langle\taup\rangle$ is the mean partition period.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Each oscillation cycle of the fundamental mode constitutes one complete
state-counting operation: the system traverses all accessible phase space
cells at that energy and returns to its starting cell (Poincar\'e recurrence,
Chapter~\ref{ch:oscillation}).  Each such cycle increments the partition depth
by one unit.  The rate of depth increment is the oscillation frequency:
$d\Depth/dt = 1/T = \omega/(2\pi)$, where $T = 2\pi/\omega$.  This equals
$1/\langle\taup\rangle$ with $\langle\taup\rangle = T$.
\end{proof}

This theorem reveals that \emph{mass spectrometry is state counting}.  Every
mass analyser works by counting oscillation cycles.  The four major analyser
types correspond to four different choices of $(\Depth, \Apartition)$, as we
now derive.

\subsection{Derivation of All Analyser Equations}
\label{sec:ch23-analyzers}

\begin{theorem}[Analyser Universality]
\label{thm:ch23-analyzers}
All four major mass analyser types---Time-of-Flight, Quadrupole,
Orbitrap, and FT-ICR---emerge from the single partition Lagrangian
\eqref{eq:ch23-lagrangian} with different choices of partition depth field
$\Depth(\mathbf{x}, t)$ and vector potential $\Apartition(\mathbf{x})$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
We derive each case explicitly.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Time-of-Flight.}
Set $\Depth_{\mathrm{TOF}}(z) = -\kappa z$ (constant gradient,
$\kappa > 0$) and $\Apartition = \mathbf{0}$.  The Lagrangian becomes
$\Lagr = \frac{1}{2}\partinert\dot{z}^2 + \kappa z$, giving uniform
acceleration $\partinert\ddot{z} = \kappa$.  Starting from rest over
distance $L$:
\[
  L = \frac{1}{2}\frac{\kappa}{\partinert}T_{\mathrm{TOF}}^2
  \implies
  \boxed{T_{\mathrm{TOF}} = \sqrt{\frac{2\alpha L}{\kappa}}\cdot\sqrt{\frac{m}{z}}
  \propto \sqrt{\frac{m}{z}}}.
\]

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Quadrupole Mass Filter.}
Set $\Depth_{\mathrm{quad}}(x,y,t) = \frac{\kappa_0}{2}(x^2-y^2)
[U + V\cos(\Omega t)]$, $\Apartition = \mathbf{0}$.  The Euler--Lagrange
equations separate into the Mathieu equation
$\ddot{x}/d\xi^2 + (a_u - 2q_u\cos 2\xi)x = 0$ (with $\xi = \Omega t/2$),
where the stability parameters are:
\[
  \boxed{a_u = \frac{4\kappa_0 U}{\alpha\Omega^2}\cdot\frac{z}{m},
  \qquad
  q_u = \frac{2\kappa_0 V}{\alpha\Omega^2}\cdot\frac{z}{m}}.
\]
Ions with $(a_u, q_u)$ in the Mathieu stability region have bounded
trajectories and pass through the filter.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Orbitrap.}
Set $\Depth_{\mathrm{orbi}}(r,z) = \frac{\kappa}{2}(z^2 - r^2/2) +
\frac{\kappa R_m^2}{2}\ln(r/R_m)$, $\Apartition = \mathbf{0}$.
The axial equation $\partinert\ddot{z} = -\kappa z$ is simple harmonic motion
with angular frequency:
\[
  \boxed{\omega_{\mathrm{orbi}} = \sqrt{\frac{\kappa}{\partinert}}
  = \sqrt{\frac{\kappa}{\alpha}}\cdot\sqrt{\frac{z}{m}}
  \propto \sqrt{\frac{z}{m}}}.
\]

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{FT-ICR.}
Set $\Depth = 0$ and $\Apartition = \frac{B}{2}(-y, x, 0)$, giving a uniform
partition curl field $\mathbf{B}_\Depth = B\hat{z}$.  The equation of motion
for circular cyclotron motion yields:
\[
  \boxed{\omega_c = \frac{zeB}{m} \propto \frac{z}{m}}.
\]

The four field configurations are the complete space of practically realisable
partition field topologies: static linear, time-dependent quadratic, static
quadradic-plus-logarithmic, and pure vector potential.
\end{proof}

\begin{consequence}{All Analyser Equations from One Lagrangian}{ch23-analyzers}
The four dominant mass analyser equations
$T \propto \sqrt{m/z}$ (TOF),
Mathieu stability (Quadrupole),
$\omega \propto \sqrt{z/m}$ (Orbitrap),
$\omega_c \propto z/m$ (FT-ICR)
are not independent empirical facts but specialisations of the single partition
Lagrangian $\Lagr_\Depth = \frac{1}{2}\partinert|\dot{\mathbf{x}}|^2 +
\partinert\dot{\mathbf{x}} \cdot \Apartition - \Depth$ with different field
topologies.  The Lagrangian is derived from the Bounded Phase Space Axiom
(Chapter~\ref{ch:axiom}); all four analyser equations are therefore
consequences of the axiom.
\end{consequence}

\section{Trajectory Determinism and On-Demand Generation}
\label{sec:ch23-determinism}

\subsection{From Addressing to Generation}
\label{sec:ch23-address-to-gen}

The ternary addressing scheme identifies a point in S-entropy space;
that point determines the vibrational frequency spectrum; the spectrum
determines $m/z$; and $m/z$ combined with the analyser geometry determines
the complete ion trajectory via the partition Lagrangian.  The entire chain
is deterministic.

\begin{theorem}[Trajectory Determinism]
\label{thm:ch23-determinism}
Given S-entropy coordinates $(\Sk, \St, \Se) \in \mathcal{S}$ at sufficient
trit depth $k$, the following are uniquely determined:
\begin{enumerate}[(a)]
  \item The vibrational frequency spectrum $\{\omega_i\}_{i=1}^{\Nvib}$.
  \item The mass-to-charge ratio $m/z$.
  \item The ion trajectory $\mathbf{x}(t)$ through any specified analyser
    geometry.
  \item The complete mass spectrum including fragmentation pattern.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(a)~The three S-entropy coordinates encode three algebraically independent
aspects of the frequency spectrum (Theorem~\ref{thm:ch23-unique-resolution}).
At sufficient trit depth, the ternary cell contains at most one compound,
uniquely identifying the frequency set $\{\omega_i\}$.

(b)~The molecular mass $M = \sum_j m_j$ is determined by the atomic
composition, which is encoded in the vibrational structure through $\omega_i =
\sqrt{k_i/\mu_i}$.  The charge state $z$ is fixed by ionisation conditions.
Hence $m/z$ is determined.

(c)~Given $m/z = \partinert/\alpha$ and analyser fields $(\Depth, \Apartition)$,
the Euler--Lagrange equations for $\Lagr_\Depth$ with Lipschitz-continuous
right-hand sides have a unique solution $\mathbf{x}(t)$ by the Cauchy--Lipschitz
theorem, given initial conditions fixed by the ionisation source.

(d)~Fragmentation patterns follow from the molecular structure encoded in the
frequency spectrum: the weakest vibrational modes (lowest frequencies) identify
preferred fragmentation sites, and the resulting fragment masses are
deterministic functions of the molecular graph.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The Generative Database}
\label{sec:ch23-gen-db}

\begin{definition}[Generative Spectral Database]
\label{def:ch23-gen-db}
A \emph{generative spectral database} is a data structure consisting of:
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
  \item The S-entropy coordinate space $\mathcal{S} = [0,1]^3$,
  \item The ternary trie $\mathcal{T}$ over $\mathcal{S}$,
  \item The partition Lagrangian $\Lagr_\Depth$ with analyser-specific fields.
\end{enumerate}
It contains \emph{no stored spectra}.  It generates any entry on demand via
Trajectory Determinism (Theorem~\ref{thm:ch23-determinism}).
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Generative Completeness]
\label{thm:ch23-completeness}
For any compound whose vibrational frequencies are known or computable from
first principles, the generative database produces the complete ion trajectory,
mass spectrum, and molecular identification without pre-storage.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Given vibrational frequencies $\{\omega_i\}$, the S-entropy coordinates are
computed by Definitions~\ref{def:ch23-sk}--\ref{def:ch23-se}.  The ternary
address is computed by the interleaving scheme of Definition~\ref{def:ch23-address}.
The trajectory is generated by Theorem~\ref{thm:ch23-determinism}.  At no
point is a stored spectrum consulted.  The entire output is generated from
the input frequencies and the fixed mathematical structure
$(\mathcal{S}, \mathcal{T}, \Lagr_\Depth)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{consequence}{O(k) Query Complexity}{ch23-okquery}
The generative spectral database requires $O(k)$ operations per query,
independent of the number of representable compounds $N$.  Specifically:
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
  \item \emph{Address resolution}: $k$ trit comparisons, each $O(1)$.
    Total: $O(k)$.
  \item \emph{Trajectory generation}: $O(1)$ for TOF and Orbitrap
    (closed-form solutions), $O(1)$ for FT-ICR (closed-form cyclotron
    frequency), $O(N_{\mathrm{cycles}})$ for Quadrupole (numerical
    integration over RF cycles).
\end{enumerate}
The total query cost $O(k)$ is independent of $N$.  At PubChem scale
($N = 10^8$, $k = 12$--$24$), the generative database provides a speedup
exceeding $10^9$ over exhaustive library search.
\end{consequence}

\begin{proof}
Each trit comparison and interval update in the address-resolution step costs
$O(1)$.  There are $k$ such steps.  The Lagrangian evaluation is a fixed-cost
operation for a given address (the field equations are evaluated at fixed
parameters).  The total cost $O(k) + O(1) = O(k)$ is independent of $N$
because no library is consulted.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Storage Elimination]
\label{cor:ch23-storage}
The generative database requires $O(1)$ storage for the physics (Lagrangian
plus coordinate definitions) plus $O(k)$ for the trie structure at depth $k$,
independent of the number of potential entries $N$.  A generative database
capable of addressing $N = 3^k$ compounds requires only the fixed physics plus
a $k$-level trie, regardless of $N$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The partition Lagrangian and S-entropy definitions are fixed mathematical
objects: $O(1)$ storage.  Each compound's address is computed from its
S-entropy coordinates, not stored in a lookup table.  The trie structure is
defined by the interleaving scheme and requires no compound-specific storage.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The Generation Protocol}
\label{sec:ch23-protocol}

The generative database responds to queries according to the following
deterministic protocol.

\begin{definition}[On-Demand Generation Protocol]
\label{def:ch23-protocol}
Given a query (as S-entropy coordinates, a partial ternary prefix, or
vibrational frequencies), the generative database produces the complete mass
spectrum as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Receive query.}  Input is one of: (a)~S-entropy coordinates
    $(\Sk, \St, \Se)$; (b)~ternary prefix $a_1 \cdots a_j$; or (c)~vibrational
    frequencies $\{\omega_i\}$.
  \item \textbf{Resolve address.}  If (a): apply the interleaving algorithm
    (Definition~\ref{def:ch23-address}).  If (b): address is partially
    specified; extend by coordinate recovery.  If (c): compute S-entropy
    coordinates via Definitions~\ref{def:ch23-sk}--\ref{def:ch23-se}, then
    compute the address.
  \item \textbf{Determine vibrational frequencies.}  From the S-entropy
    coordinates, recover $\{\omega_i\}$ using the inverse map (shape from $\Sk$,
    scale from $\St$, coupling from $\Se$).
  \item \textbf{Compute $m/z$.}  From the frequency spectrum and atomic masses,
    compute $M$ and the charge state $z$ (from the ionisation protocol).
  \item \textbf{Select analyser geometry.}  Choose $(\Depth, \Apartition)$
    for the target analyser (TOF, Quadrupole, Orbitrap, FT-ICR).
  \item \textbf{Instantiate partition Lagrangian.}  Construct
    $\Lagr_\Depth = \tfrac{1}{2}\partinert|\dot{\mathbf{x}}|^2 +
    \partinert\dot{\mathbf{x}} \cdot \Apartition - \Depth$ with
    $\partinert = \alpha(m/z)$.
  \item \textbf{Solve equations of motion.}  Integrate
    $\partinert\ddot{\mathbf{x}} = -\nabla\Depth +
    \partinert\dot{\mathbf{x}} \times \mathbf{B}_\Depth$ with specified
    initial conditions.
  \item \textbf{Output.}  Return the complete mass spectrum (parent ion $m/z$,
    fragment ions, relative abundances), the trajectory $\mathbf{x}(t)$, and
    the analyser observable (flight time, stability, oscillation frequency).
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\subsection{Virtual Detector Lifecycle}
\label{sec:ch23-virtual-detector}

\begin{definition}[Virtual Detector]
\label{def:ch23-vd}
A \emph{virtual detector} is a measurement device that exists only during
the generation process.  It materialises from the phase space structure,
reads the categorical state (the S-entropy coordinates), and dissolves after
the measurement is complete.  The entry exists only during the query, and
only then.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Virtual Detector Lifecycle]
\label{thm:ch23-vd-lifecycle}
The virtual detector has three phases:
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
  \item \emph{Materialisation} $O(1)$: instantiation of the coordinate system
    and trie structure.
  \item \emph{Measurement} $O(K)$: traversal of $K = 14$ dimensions (three
    S-entropy coordinates resolved to depth $k$, plus ancillary parameters
    including $\Nvib$, molecular mass, charge state, and analyser fields).
  \item \emph{Dissolution} $O(1)$: release of the instantiated structure.
\end{enumerate}
Total existence: milliseconds on modern hardware.  Total cost: $O(K) = O(1)$
for fixed $K$.
\end{theorem}

\section{The Ion-Droplet Bijection}
\label{sec:ch23-bijection}

\subsection{Dual Oscillatory Representation}
\label{sec:ch23-dual}

The vibrational frequency spectrum provides one oscillatory representation of
molecular identity (frequency domain).  A second, geometrically dual
representation exists in the spatial domain: the surface-wave modes of a droplet.

\begin{definition}[Ion Representation]
\label{def:ch23-ion-repr}
The \emph{ion representation} of a molecular compound is its vibrational
frequency spectrum $\{\omega_i\}_{i=1}^{\Nvib}$ mapped to S-entropy
coordinates $(\Sk, \St, \Se)$ via Definitions~\ref{def:ch23-sk}--\ref{def:ch23-se}.
The identification path traverses the ternary trie in frequency space.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Droplet Representation]
\label{def:ch23-drip-repr}
The \emph{droplet representation} of a molecular compound is the set of
surface-wave modes $\{f_j\}_{j=1}^{N_{\mathrm{modes}}}$ of a spherical
droplet with radius $R \propto \sigma_{\mathrm{mol}}^{1/2}$ (from the
molecular collision cross-section), surface tension $\gamma \propto$ bond
energy density, and density $\rho \propto M/V_{\mathrm{mol}}$.  The Rayleigh
surface displacement expands in spherical harmonics:
\[
  r(\theta,\phi,t) = R + \sum_{l=0}^\infty \sum_{m=-l}^{l}
    a_{lm}(t)\,Y_l^m(\theta,\phi),
\]
with capillary frequencies:
\[
  f_l = \sqrt{\frac{l(l-1)(l+2)\gamma}{\rho R^3}}, \quad l \geq 2.
\]
These frequencies $\{f_l\}$ are mapped to S-entropy coordinates via
Definitions~\ref{def:ch23-sk}--\ref{def:ch23-se} applied to the droplet
spectrum.
\end{definition}

\subsection{Bijective Transformation Proof}
\label{sec:ch23-bijection-proof}

\begin{theorem}[Ion-Droplet Bijection]
\label{thm:ch23-bijection}
There exists a bijective map $\mathcal{D} : \mathcal{I} \to \mathcal{D}$
from the space of molecular ions $\mathcal{I}$ to the space of representative
droplets $\mathcal{D}$ such that:
\begin{enumerate}[(a)]
  \item Spectral information is completely preserved (no information loss).
  \item Both representations encode identical S-entropy coordinates:
    $(\Sk, \St, \Se)_{\mathrm{ion}} = (\Sk, \St, \Se)_{\mathrm{drip}}$.
  \item The transformation is invertible.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Construction.}  For a molecular ion with mass $m$, kinetic energy $E_k$,
collision cross-section $\sigma$, and mode couplings $\{c_{ij}\}$, define
the droplet by:
\begin{align*}
  R &= \sqrt{\sigma/\pi}, &
  \rho &= 3m/(4\pi R^3), \\
  v &= \sqrt{2E_k/m}, &
  \gamma &= \gamma_0 \cdot f(\{c_{ij}\}),
\end{align*}
where $f$ is a monotonic function mapping coupling coefficients to surface
tension (stronger bonds $\to$ higher surface tension).

\emph{Injectivity.}  Suppose $\mathcal{D}(\text{Ion}_1) = \mathcal{D}(\text{Ion}_2)$.
Then $R_1 = R_2$ (implying $\sigma_1 = \sigma_2$), $v_1 = v_2$ (implying
$E_{k,1}/m_1 = E_{k,2}/m_2$), $\rho_1 = \rho_2$ (implying $m_1 = m_2$ since
$R_1 = R_2$), and $\gamma_1 = \gamma_2$ (implying identical coupling networks).
So Ion$_1$ and Ion$_2$ have the same mass, cross-section, kinetic energy, and
bonding network, hence are the same molecular species.

\emph{Surjectivity.}  For any physically realisable droplet
($R, \rho, \gamma, v > 0$), invert: $\sigma = \pi R^2$,
$m = \tfrac{4}{3}\pi R^3\rho$, $E_k = \tfrac{1}{2}mv^2$,
$\{c_{ij}\} = f^{-1}(\gamma/\gamma_0)$.  The resulting ion is physically
realisable.

\emph{S-entropy preservation.}  The ion spectrum $\{\omega_i\}$ and the
droplet spectrum $\{f_l\}$ are both frequency sets encoding the same physical
system.  Mass determines frequency scale (heavier molecules $\to$ lower
frequencies, larger droplets $\to$ lower capillary frequencies, both scaling
as $\omega \propto m^{-1/2}$).  Coupling structure determines anharmonic
interactions in both representations.  Since S-entropy coordinates depend only
on normalised distributions and ratios:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $\Sk$: the normalised distributions $\{p_i\}$ are identical because
    $p_i = \omega_i/\sum\omega_j = f_i/\sum f_j$ under monotonic scaling.
  \item $\St$: the dynamic range $\omega_{\max}/\omega_{\min} = f_{\max}/f_{\min}$
    is preserved under monotonic scaling.
  \item $\Se$: harmonic proximity ratios $\omega_a/\omega_b = f_a/f_b$
    are preserved under uniform scaling.
\end{itemize}
Therefore $S_{\mathrm{ion}} = S_{\mathrm{drip}}$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Dual Path Convergence}
\label{sec:ch23-convergence}

\begin{theorem}[Dual Path Convergence]
\label{thm:ch23-dual-convergence}
Ion-path identification (traversing the trie using vibrational frequencies)
and drip-path identification (traversing the trie using droplet surface modes)
converge to the same ternary address:
\[
  \mathrm{addr}(S_{\mathrm{ion}}) = \mathrm{addr}(S_{\mathrm{drip}}).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By the Ion-Droplet Bijection (Theorem~\ref{thm:ch23-bijection}(b)),
$S_{\mathrm{ion}} = S_{\mathrm{drip}}$.  The ternary encoding is a
deterministic function of the S-entropy coordinates.  Therefore the same
input produces the same output.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Algorithm-Free Cross-Validation]
\label{cor:ch23-validation}
Agreement between dual-path identifications provides independent validation
without any algorithmic comparison.  The probability of false agreement
(two different compounds producing the same address on both paths by
coincidence) is bounded by $P_{\mathrm{false}} \leq 3^{-k}$ at depth $k$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
At depth $k$, there are $3^k$ possible addresses.  A different molecule
has a different ion-path address (by injectivity of the S-entropy map,
Theorem~\ref{thm:ch23-unique-resolution}).  The only failure mode is
insufficient resolution (two molecules in the same depth-$k$ cell).  The
probability of this under a uniform prior is $3^{-k}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{consequence}{The Ion-Droplet Bijection as Dual Validation}{ch23-bijection}
The bijection $\mathcal{D}$ between molecular ions and representative droplets
provides two independent oscillatory paths to the same S-entropy coordinates.
Both paths are physical (one uses vibrational spectroscopy, the other uses
capillary wave dynamics).  Both paths are algorithm-free (comparison is
address matching, not similarity computation).  Agreement of the two paths
constitutes cross-validation with false-positive probability at most $3^{-k}$.
\end{consequence}

\section{The Empty Database}
\label{sec:ch23-empty}

\subsection{What the Database Contains}
\label{sec:ch23-contains}

The generative spectral database contains nothing.

It is not an empty container waiting to be filled.  It is the coordinate
system itself: the S-entropy space $\mathcal{S} = [0,1]^3$, the ternary trie
$\mathcal{T}$ that discretises this space, and the partition Lagrangian
$\Lagr_\Depth$ that generates trajectories.  These three mathematical
objects---a compact metric space, a hierarchical addressing scheme, and a
dynamical law---constitute the entire database.

When a query arrives, the database does not look up a stored entry.  It
\emph{generates} the entry from the query's S-entropy coordinates via the
partition Lagrangian, outputs the result, and discards it.  The entry exists
only during the generation process.  Before the query, it does not exist.
After the output, it does not exist.  The database is empty at all times.

\begin{theorem}[Phase Space Identity]
\label{thm:ch23-phase-space}
The generative spectral database \emph{is} a discretised representation of
bounded phase space $\mathcal{S} = [0,1]^3$.  Nodes of the trie represent
physical regions of phase space.  Edges represent refinement operations
(measurement acts).  The trie \emph{is} the physics.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By the Trit--Cell Bijection (Theorem~\ref{thm:ch23-trit-cell}), each $k$-trit
string identifies a unique cell in the partition of $[0,1]^3$.  Each cell
corresponds to a region of S-entropy space containing all compounds whose
S-coordinates fall within its boundaries.  The trie edges (parent $\to$ child)
correspond to refinement operations that further resolve identity.  By
Position--Trajectory Duality (Theorem~\ref{thm:ch23-duality}), traversing an
edge \emph{is} performing a measurement.  The trie structure \emph{is} the
hierarchical partition of phase space.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Address Stability]
\label{cor:ch23-stability}
Ternary addresses are determined by physics (vibrational spectra), not by
database contents.  Adding or removing entries changes nothing about existing
addresses, because the coordinate system $\mathcal{S}$ and the Lagrangian
$\Lagr_\Depth$ are fixed mathematical structures independent of the population
of any lookup table.
\end{corollary}

within numerical precision (relative difference $< 10^{-6}$), confirming the

\section{Complexity Analysis}
\label{sec:ch23-complexity}

\subsection{Traditional Search: $O(N \times d)$}
\label{sec:ch23-trad}

In a traditional spectral database with $N$ stored spectra each of dimension
$d$, a query requires comparing the unknown spectrum against every stored
spectrum.  Each comparison costs $O(d)$ (evaluating a cosine similarity or dot
product over $d$-dimensional vectors).  Total query cost: $O(N \times d)$.
Even with locality-sensitive hashing or kd-trees, the worst-case complexity
remains polynomial in $N$, and the curse of dimensionality renders indexing
ineffective for high-dimensional spectral data ($d \sim 10^3$--$10^4$).

\subsection{Generative Database: $O(k)$}
\label{sec:ch23-gen-complexity}

The generative database performs two operations per query:
(i)~address resolution at cost $O(k)$; (ii)~trajectory generation at cost
$O(1)$ for TOF, Orbitrap, and FT-ICR (closed-form solutions) or
$O(N_{\mathrm{cycles}})$ for Quadrupole (numerical integration over RF cycles).
The total query cost $O(k)$ is independent of $N$.

\subsection{What Was Demonstrated}

\end{enumerate}

The entire chain---from axiom to functioning database---is self-contained.
No external chemical knowledge, no pre-stored spectra, and no machine-learning
models are required.  The database is the physics; the physics is the database.

\subsection{The Database That Contains Nothing}
\label{sec:ch23-nothing}

The philosophical implications extend beyond computational efficiency.
Traditional databases embody a \emph{correspondence} theory of truth: a query
matches a stored entry if and only if they correspond (similarity score exceeds
threshold).  The generative database embodies a \emph{coherence} theory: a
query is identified not by matching a stored pattern but by generating the
unique trajectory consistent with the query's S-entropy coordinates.

This distinction matters because the generative approach can identify compounds
that have \emph{never been measured}.  A traditional database is limited to its
contents; the generative database is limited only by the validity of the physics.
Any compound that exists as a stable molecule (satisfying the Bounded Phase
Space Axiom) can be generated and identified.

The database is empty not because it lacks data but because it does not need
data.  The right representation---one that encodes the physics rather than the
outcomes---eliminates the need for storage entirely.

\subsection{From Counting to Chemistry}
\label{sec:ch23-counting}

The fundamental identity $d\Depth/dt = \omega/(2\pi)$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:ch23-fundamental}) reveals that mass spectrometry is a
counting process.  Each oscillation cycle---whether in a TOF drift tube, a
quadrupole RF field, an Orbitrap axial potential, or an ICR magnetic
field---is one tick of a clock that counts the system's state transitions.

In the ternary encoding, each trit is one refinement: one observation that
distinguishes one third of phase space from the other two thirds.  The
$k$-trit address records $k$ such observations.  The address \emph{is} the
measurement record.  The encoding \emph{is} the measurement.  The position
in the trie \emph{is} the molecular identity.

This dissolves the traditional separation between measurement and
identification.  In conventional mass spectrometry, measurement produces a
spectrum and identification matches it against a database.  In the generative
framework, measurement \emph{is} identification: each trit of the ternary
address simultaneously locates the compound in S-entropy space and identifies
which compound it is.

\subsection{Falsifiable Predictions}
\label{sec:ch23-predictions}

The framework generates the following falsifiable predictions:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{Trajectory determinism:} Any compound's trajectory is
    deterministically generated from its S-entropy coordinates with the
    partition Lagrangian.  A compound whose experimental trajectory deviates
    from the generated trajectory (beyond instrument precision) would falsify
    the framework.
  \item \emph{Spectral accuracy:} Generated spectra match experimental
    spectra to within partition depth resolution.  Systematic deviations
    would indicate missing physics in the Lagrangian.
  \item \emph{Bijection preservation:} Finding a compound where ion-path
    and drip-path identifications diverge at sufficient trit depth would
    falsify the bijection.
  \item \emph{$O(k)$ scaling:} Query cost is independent of database size.
    If query cost increases with $N$ beyond $O(k)$, the generative model
    is incomplete.
  \item \emph{Chemical cohesion:} Chemical families show ternary cohesion
    ($R > 1$) without chemical knowledge.  A chemical family whose members are
    dispersed in ternary space would indicate that S-entropy coordinates fail to
    capture chemical similarity.
  \item \emph{Analyser universality:} All four analyser types emerge from the
    partition Lagrangian.  Discovery of a mass analyser whose behaviour cannot
    be expressed as a specialisation of \eqref{eq:ch23-lagrangian} would
    require extending the Lagrangian.
\end{enumerate}
