\section{The Aufbau Principle}

\begin{consequence}{Aufbau (Shell-Filling) Principle}{aufbau}
  Electrons fill partition cells in order of increasing partition depth $n$,
  and within each $n$ in order of increasing $l$.
  Each cell holds at most one electron (Consequence~\ref{cons:pauli}).
\end{consequence}

The Aufbau principle is not a rule imposed from spectroscopy but a consequence
of the exclusion principle (one particle per cell) combined with energy
ordering: lower partition depth $n$ corresponds to smaller $\Depth$ (higher
stability), so the ground state fills cells from $n = 1$ upward.

\section{Slater Shielding from Radial Integrals}

\begin{consequence}{Slater Shielding}{slater}
  The effective nuclear charge experienced by an electron in shell $n$ is
  \begin{equation}
    Z^*(n) = Z - \sigma(n),
  \end{equation}
  where $\sigma(n)$ is the shielding constant computed from the radial integrals
  of the partition cells at depths $< n$.
\end{consequence}

Slater's empirical rules (1930) are the approximate version of the exact
partition-integral shielding constants.
The partition framework gives the exact result; Slater's rules are the leading
approximation.

\section{Atomic Emptiness}

\begin{consequence}{Atomic Emptiness}{atomicemptiness}
  The fraction of an atom's classical volume occupied by its electrons is
  \begin{equation}
    f_\mathrm{occ} = \frac{\sum_n C_\mathrm{occ}(n)\,V_\mathrm{cell}(n)}{V_\mathrm{atom}}
    \sim \left(\frac{r_e}{a_0}\right)^3 \approx 10^{-15},
  \end{equation}
  where $r_e$ is the classical electron radius and $a_0$ is the Bohr radius.
\end{consequence}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
  From the Loschmidt principle (Consequence~\ref{cons:loschmidt}): each
  electron's visited-cell set $\mathcal{V}$ is a bounded subset of the
  atomic partition hierarchy.
  The cell volume $V_\mathrm{cell}(n) \sim (a_0/n)^3$.
  The ratio of occupied to total volume is $(r_e/a_0)^3$ by the rest-mass
  relation $m_e c^2 = e^2/(4\pi\varepsilon_0 r_e) = \hbar\omega_0$.
\end{proof}

Rutherford's experimental discovery that atoms are mostly empty is a theorem
of partition geometry.
