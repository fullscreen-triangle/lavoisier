\section{The Central Thesis: Forces Are Not Fundamental}
\label{sec:ch21-thesis}
%==============================================================================

Every mass spectrometry textbook describes the instrument through forces.  The
ion source applies an electric field.  Ion lenses use electromagnetic potentials.
The mass analyser employs the Lorentz force $\mathbf{F} = q(\mathbf{E}+\mathbf{v}
\times\mathbf{B})$ to separate ions by $m/z$.  The detector registers
mechanical impact.  Trajectory simulation codes integrate Newton's second law
through field geometries.

But what \emph{are} these forces?  Why does the Lorentz force take its
particular form?  Why does mass resist acceleration?  Why does charge exist?
The standard framework treats mass, charge, and force as primitive quantities
with empirical values.  This chapter demonstrates they are unnecessary.

The derivation proceeds from a single axiom---the Bounded Phase Space Law
established in Chapter~\ref{ch:axiom}---without invoking any force.  The
central mechanism is \emph{partition depth minimisation}: ions are incomplete
categorical structures (partition malformations) that evolve toward partition
completion by descending the partition depth gradient.  What is called an
``electric field'' is a partition depth gradient.  What is called the
``mass-to-charge ratio'' is partition inertia.  What is called a ``detector
signal'' is a partition completion event.  No force acts at any stage.

\begin{consequence}{Forces Are Partition Gradients}{forces-gradients}
  All four ``fundamental forces'' acting on ions in a mass spectrometer are
  gradients or transitions of the partition depth field $\Depth(\mathbf{x},t)$.
  The Lorentz force is the Euler--Lagrange equation for partition depth
  minimisation.  Forces add no predictive power; they obscure the underlying
  partition structure.
\end{consequence}

\subsection{Gravity as Partition Depth Gradient}

From the derivation in Chapter~\ref{ch:mass}, any system in bounded phase space

\label{thm:ch21-gravity}

  \end{equation}

\begin{proof}

  stops because $\Depth$ is minimised, not because forces balance.  The
  coefficient $\kB T\ln b$ converts depth units to energy units by the
  partition entropy identity $\Delta S_{\mathcal{P}} = \kB \ln b \cdot

\section{Ions as Partition Malformations}
\label{sec:ch21-malformation}

A neutral atom has complete partition structure: every electron occupies a
shell slot, and $\Depth = \Depth_{\min}$.  Ionisation disrupts this
completeness.

\begin{definition}[Partition Malformation]
  A cation (electron deficit) has unfilled partition slots; an anion has
  excess occupants.  In either case $\Depth_{\mathrm{ion}} > \Depth_{\min}$.
  The ion is thermodynamically unstable with respect to its partition structure
  and evolves to minimise $\Depth$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Partition Inertia]
  $\mu \equiv \alpha(m/z)$ is the resistance to restructuring partition
  coordinates.  Mass IS partition inertia---not a separate quantity that
  ``responds to force.''  The coefficient $\alpha$ converts dimensional units;
  the ratio $m/z$ encodes both partition inertia ($m$) and malformation charge
  ($z$).

The partition inertia $\mu$ plays the role of mass in the equations of motion:
heavier ions (larger $\mu$) descend the partition depth gradient more slowly,
giving rise to all mass-dependent separations observed in the four analyser
technologies.

\section{The Partition Lagrangian}
\label{sec:ch21-lagrangian}

Chapter~\ref{ch:lagrangian} derived the partition Lagrangian from the Bounded
Phase Space Axiom via Galilean invariance.  We collect the key results here as
they apply to the force-free ion.

\begin{theorem}[Partition Lagrangian]
\label{thm:ch21-lagrangian}
  The Lagrangian for an ion with partition inertia $\mu = \alpha(m/z)$ in
  the partition depth field $\Depth(\mathbf{x},t)$ with vector potential
  $\Apartition(\mathbf{x})$ is:
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:ch21-lagrangian}
    \boxed{
      \Lagr_\Depth = \frac{1}{2}\mu|\dot{\mathbf{x}}|^2
                   + \mu\dot{\mathbf{x}}\cdot\Apartition(\mathbf{x})
                   - \Depth(\mathbf{x}, t).
    }
  \end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  Galilean invariance requires the unique quadratic kinetic term
  $\frac{1}{2}\mu|\dot{\mathbf{x}}|^2$.  Coupling to the scalar potential
  $\Depth$ and the vector potential $\Apartition$ gives the minimal Lagrangian
  satisfying: (i) correct equations of motion via Euler--Lagrange; (ii)
  recovery of all four analyser equations as topology specialisations; (iii)
  no force terms.
\end{proof}

Applying the Euler--Lagrange procedure to $\Lagr_\Depth$:

\begin{theorem}[Force-Free Equations of Motion]
\label{thm:ch21-eom}
  The equations of motion derived from $\Lagr_\Depth$ are:
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:ch21-eom}
    \mu\ddot{\mathbf{x}} = -\nabla\Depth + \mu\dot{\mathbf{x}}\times\mathbf{B}_\Depth,
  \end{equation}
  where $\mathbf{B}_\Depth = \nabla\times\Apartition$.  This has the
  \emph{structure} of the Lorentz force but IS partition depth minimisation.
  The ``electric field'' is $-\nabla\Depth/\mu$; the ``magnetic field'' is
  $\mathbf{B}_\Depth$.  No force concept is required.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  Compute $\partial\Lagr_\Depth/\partial\dot{x}_i = \mu\dot{x}_i +
  \mu A_i$ and $\partial\Lagr_\Depth/\partial x_i = \mu\dot{x}_j\,
  \partial A_j/\partial x_i - \partial\Depth/\partial x_i$.  The Euler--Lagrange
  equation $d/dt(\partial\Lagr/\partial\dot{x}_i) = \partial\Lagr/\partial x_i$
  then gives $\mu\ddot{x}_i = -\partial\Depth/\partial x_i +
  \mu(\dot{x}_j\,\partial A_j/\partial x_i - \dot{x}_j\,\partial A_i/\partial
  x_j)$, which in vector form is Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch21-eom} with
  $(\mathbf{B}_\Depth)_k = \epsilon_{kij}\,\partial A_j/\partial x_i$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
  Equation~\eqref{eq:ch21-eom} is identical to the Lorentz force equation with
  the replacements $q\mathbf{E} \to -\nabla\Depth$ and $q\mathbf{v}\times
  \mathbf{B} \to \mu\dot{\mathbf{x}}\times\mathbf{B}_\Depth$.  This is not a
  coincidence: the Lorentz force \emph{is} the partition depth gradient,
  expressed in the language of forces.  No additional content is added by the
  force description.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Time-of-Flight Analyser}

\paragraph{Partition topology.}
$\Depth_{\mathrm{TOF}}(z) = -\kappa z$, $\Apartition = \mathbf{0}$.  The
partition depth field is a linear gradient along the flight axis.

\paragraph{Derivation.}
Equation~\eqref{eq:ch21-eom} reduces to $\mu\ddot{z} = \kappa$ (uniform
acceleration from a constant partition gradient).  Starting from rest at $z=0$:
$z(t) = \kappa t^2 / (2\mu)$.  Setting $z = L$ (the flight tube length):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ch21-tof}
  \boxed{T_{\mathrm{TOF}} = \sqrt{\frac{2\mu L}{\kappa}} = \sqrt{\frac{2\alpha L}{\kappa}}\cdot\sqrt{m/z}\;\propto\;\sqrt{m/z}.}
\end{equation}
Flight time is proportional to $\sqrt{m/z}$, the fundamental TOF scaling law.
No electric force, no kinetic energy argument---only partition gradient
descent.

\paragraph{Numerical example.}
For caffeine ($m/z = 195.09$, singly charged) and reserpine ($m/z = 609.28$)
in a 1-m TOF with $\kappa/\mu = 2\times 10^6\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$:
\begin{align*}
  T_{\mathrm{TOF}}(\text{caffeine})  &= \sqrt{2\times 1/(2\times 10^6)}\cdot\sqrt{195.09}
    \approx 13.96\;\mu\mathrm{s},\\
  T_{\mathrm{TOF}}(\text{reserpine}) &= \sqrt{2\times 1/(2\times 10^6)}\cdot\sqrt{609.28}
    \approx 24.68\;\mu\mathrm{s}.
\end{align*}
Ratio: $24.68/13.96 = 1.768$; $\sqrt{609.28/195.09} = 1.767$.  Agreement to
$<0.1\%$.
