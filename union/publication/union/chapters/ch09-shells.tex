\section{Shell Filling from $C(n) = 2n^2$}

The shell capacity formula derived in Chapter~\ref{ch:coordinates},
\begin{equation}
  C(n) = 2n^2,
\end{equation}
immediately predicts the filling order of atomic electron shells:
$C(1) = 2$ (helium closes the first shell),
$C(2) = 8$ (neon closes the second),
$C(3) = 18$ (argon closes the third), etc.

The first-row noble gases $\{2, 10, 18, 36, 54, 86\}$ are cumulative sums
$\sum_{k=1}^n 2k^2$, in exact agreement with experiment.

\section{Electric Dipole Selection Rules}

The selection rules of Chapter~\ref{ch:coordinates} are realised spectroscopically
as the electric dipole transition rules.
Between states $(n_1, l_1, m_1, s_1)$ and $(n_2, l_2, m_2, s_2)$:
\begin{align}
  \Delta l &= l_2 - l_1 = \pm 1, \\
  \Delta m &= m_2 - m_1 \in \{-1, 0, +1\}, \\
  \Delta s &= 0 \quad\text{(for electric dipole)}.
\end{align}
Higher-order transitions (magnetic dipole: $\Delta l = 0$; electric quadrupole:
$\Delta l = \pm 2$) correspond to higher-rank tensor operators in the
Wigner--Eckart theorem.

\section{Spectroscopic Applications}

The hydrogen atom spectrum follows directly.
Transition frequencies $\nu_{n_1 \to n_2}$ are:
\begin{equation}
  h\nu_{n_1 \to n_2} = \hbar\omega_0\left(\frac{1}{n_1^2} - \frac{1}{n_2^2}\right),
\end{equation}
where $\omega_0$ is the hydrogen ground state frequency.
This is the Rydberg formula, derived here from partition counting without
postulating the Bohr model.
