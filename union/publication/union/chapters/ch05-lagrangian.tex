\epigraph{%
  The equation of motion is not a force law imposed from outside.
  It is gradient descent on the partition depth,
  written in the alphabet of calculus of variations.%
}{\textit{On the necessity of the Lagrangian form}}

\noindent
Part~I of this monograph established the mathematical scaffolding:
partition coordinates, shell capacity, selection rules, Pauli exclusion,
and the Koopman spectral structure of bounded recurrent dynamics.
Part~II turns to dynamics proper.
This chapter derives the unique Lagrangian consistent with the Bounded Phase
Space Law (Axiom~\ref{ax:bpsl}) from first principles, without assuming
Newton's second law or the existence of forces.

The argument has four steps:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
  \item Introduce the \emph{partition depth field} $\Depth(\mathbf{x},t)$
        --- the scalar field that the system naturally extremises.
  \item Introduce the \emph{partition vector potential} $\Apartition(\mathbf{x})$
        --- the unique divergence-free vector field encoding partition vorticity.
  \item Write the unique Lagrangian coupling a label carrier to these fields
        that is consistent with Galilean invariance and completeness of
        scalar-plus-vector couplings (Consequence~\ref{cons:partitionlagrangian}).
  \item Derive the Euler--Lagrange equation and show that it has the
        Lorentz-force shape but is force-free in the electromagnetic sense.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent
Noether's theorem then yields the familiar conservation laws of Hamiltonian
mechanics --- momentum, angular momentum, energy --- as partition-depth
symmetries, not as independent postulates.

% ============================================================
\section{The Partition Depth Field and the Partition Vector Potential}
\label{sec:partitionlagrangian}
% ============================================================

\begin{definition}[Partition depth field]
\label{def:depthfield}
For each point $\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}$ and time $t$, the
\emph{partition depth field} $\Depth(\mathbf{x},t)$ is the principal depth
index $n(\mathbf{x},t)$ of the partition hierarchy extended continuously to
$\mathbb{R}^{3}$ by
\[
  \Depth(\mathbf{x},t)
    := -\kB T\ln\!\left(\frac{\mu\!\left(C_{n(\mathbf{x},t)}(\mathbf{x})\right)}%
                              {\mu(\Omega)}\right),
\]
where $C_{n}(\mathbf{x})$ is the cell of $\Partition_{n}$ containing
$\mathbf{x}$, $\mu$ is the invariant measure of Axiom~\ref{ax:bpsl}, and $T$
is the temperature parameter of the partition ensemble.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Physical reading of $\Depth$]
The quantity $\Depth(\mathbf{x},t)$ is the free-energy cost of localising
the system to the partition cell containing $\mathbf{x}$ at depth $n$.
A deep partition cell (small measure) carries a large depth; a shallow cell
(large measure) carries a small depth.
The system, driven by measure-preserving dynamics, descends toward smaller
$\Depth$ values --- toward states that occupy more of the available phase
volume.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[Partition vector potential]
\label{def:partitionA}
The \emph{partition vector potential} $\Apartition(\mathbf{x})$ is the unique
divergence-free vector field on $\mathbb{R}^{3}$ such that
$\nabla\times\Apartition$ equals the local vorticity of the partition
flow --- specifically, the antisymmetric part of the Jacobian of the
partition re-labelling map under infinitesimal time evolution.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Gauge freedom of $\Apartition$]
\label{rem:gauge}
The vector potential $\Apartition$ is defined only up to addition of
the gradient of an arbitrary smooth function $\chi(\mathbf{x},t)$: the
replacement $\Apartition\to\Apartition+\nabla\chi$ leaves the vorticity
$\nabla\times\Apartition$ unchanged because $\nabla\times\nabla\chi=0$.
This gauge freedom is not postulated; it is a direct consequence of the
definition via the curl.
\end{remark}

\section{The Partition Lagrangian}
\label{sec:lagrangian-main}
% ============================================================

\begin{consequence}{Partition Lagrangian}{partitionlagrangian}
The evolution of a partition label carrier of inertial parameter $\mu>0$
under the partition depth field admits the Lagrangian formulation
\begin{equation}
  \Lagr_{\Depth}(\mathbf{x},\dot{\mathbf{x}},t)
    = \tfrac{1}{2}\mu|\dot{\mathbf{x}}|^{2}
      + \mu\,\dot{\mathbf{x}}\cdot\Apartition(\mathbf{x})
      - \Depth(\mathbf{x},t),
  \label{eq:partitionlagrangian}
\end{equation}
with Euler--Lagrange equation
\begin{equation}
  \mu\ddot{\mathbf{x}}
    = -\nabla\Depth(\mathbf{x},t)
      + \mu\,\dot{\mathbf{x}}\times\!\left(\nabla\times\Apartition\right)\!(\mathbf{x}).
  \label{eq:partitionel}
\end{equation}
\end{consequence}

\begin{proof}
We derive~\eqref{eq:partitionlagrangian} from four constraints imposed by
Axiom~\ref{ax:bpsl} and the requirement that $\Lagr_{\Depth}$ produce
well-posed equations of motion.

\smallskip
\noindent\textit{Kinetic term.}
The only Galilean-invariant, velocity-quadratic, boundedness-preserving
scalar is $\tfrac{1}{2}\mu|\dot{\mathbf{x}}|^{2}$, up to multiplicative
constants absorbed into $\mu$.
Higher-order velocity terms (quartic, etc.) either violate Galilean
invariance (for velocity-dependent terms that do not transform as scalars)
or violate the boundedness of kinetic energy on bounded domains
(for terms that blow up as $v\to c$).
The relativistic generalisation $\mu c^{2}(1-\sqrt{1-v^{2}/c^{2}})$ reduces
to $\tfrac{1}{2}\mu v^{2}$ in the non-relativistic limit.

\smallskip
\noindent\textit{Scalar coupling.}
The only velocity-independent scalar coupling to an external field
$\Depth(\mathbf{x},t)$ is $-\Depth(\mathbf{x},t)$, with the sign fixed by
the Euler--Lagrange requirement that gradient descent on $\Depth$ be
attractive.
The sign is forced by the requirement that a depth minimum be a dynamical
attractor.

\smallskip
\noindent\textit{Vector coupling.}
The only velocity-linear, gauge-invariant coupling of a vector field
$\Apartition$ to the trajectory is $\mu\,\dot{\mathbf{x}}\cdot\Apartition$.
A term $\mu(\dot{\mathbf{x}}\cdot\Apartition)^{2}$ or higher violates
velocity-linearity; a term $|\Apartition|^{2}$ without a velocity factor is
absorbable into $\Depth$; a term with a second derivative of $\Apartition$
is not a linear coupling.

\smallskip
\noindent\textit{Completeness.}
We have exhausted the space of couplings of a position-velocity degree of
freedom to a scalar-plus-vector background.
Any coupling must be a polynomial in $(\mathbf{x},\dot{\mathbf{x}},\Depth,
\Apartition)$; velocity-constant terms give scalar coupling, velocity-linear
terms give vector coupling, velocity-quadratic terms are either kinetic
(with coefficient $\mu$) or absorbable.
The three terms in~\eqref{eq:partitionlagrangian} are the complete list.

Summing the three pieces gives~\eqref{eq:partitionlagrangian}.
The Euler--Lagrange equation from
$\tfrac{d}{dt}\tfrac{\partial\Lagr_{\Depth}}{\partial\dot{x}^{i}}
  - \tfrac{\partial\Lagr_{\Depth}}{\partial x^{i}}=0$,
after standard manipulations of the $\dot{\mathbf{x}}\cdot\Apartition$
coupling, yields~\eqref{eq:partitionel}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Structural interpretation of \eqref{eq:partitionel}]
Equation~\eqref{eq:partitionel} has the mathematical form of the Lorentz
force law of electromagnetism.
No claim is made here that the partition depth field $\Depth$ \emph{is}
the electromagnetic scalar potential, nor that $\Apartition$ is the
electromagnetic vector potential.
The point is purely structural: the equation of motion of any label carrier
over a scalar-plus-vector partition background has this shape.
The physical content of $\Depth$ in specific applications is fixed by
further Consequences (the emergence of Coulomb's law from charge as a
boundary property is derived in Chapter~\ref{ch:charge}).
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Gauge invariance as a deduced, not postulated, symmetry]
By Remark~\ref{rem:gauge}, the replacement
$\Apartition\to\Apartition+\nabla\chi$ leaves~\eqref{eq:partitionel}
invariant because $\nabla\times\nabla\chi=0$.
Equivalently, the Lagrangian changes by a total time derivative
$\mu\dot{\mathbf{x}}\cdot\nabla\chi=\mu\tfrac{d\chi}{dt}$, which does not
affect the equations of motion.
Gauge invariance appears here not as a postulated symmetry but as a
consequence of the vector-coupling structure forced on the Lagrangian by
Galilean invariance and completeness of linear couplings.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Explicit Euler--Lagrange Calculation}
\label{subsec:elcalc}
% ============================================================

We derive~\eqref{eq:partitionel} explicitly in coordinates.
The canonical momentum conjugate to $x^{i}$ is
\begin{equation*}
  p_{i}
    = \frac{\partial\Lagr_{\Depth}}{\partial\dot{x}^{i}}
    = \mu\dot{x}^{i} + \mu A_{i},
\end{equation*}
where $A_{i}$ are the Cartesian components of $\Apartition$.
The Euler--Lagrange equation is
\begin{equation*}
  \frac{d}{dt}\!\left(\mu\dot{x}^{i}+\mu A_{i}\right)
    - \frac{\partial\Lagr_{\Depth}}{\partial x^{i}} = 0.
\end{equation*}
Computing the partial derivative:
\[
  \frac{\partial\Lagr_{\Depth}}{\partial x^{i}}
    = \mu\dot{x}^{j}\partial_{i}A_{j} - \partial_{i}\Depth.
\]
Expanding the time derivative of $\mu A_{i}$ via the chain rule
($\dot{A}_{i} = \dot{x}^{j}\partial_{j}A_{i}$) and rearranging:
\begin{equation*}
  \mu\ddot{x}^{i}
    = -\partial_{i}\Depth
      + \mu\dot{x}^{j}\!\left(\partial_{i}A_{j}-\partial_{j}A_{i}\right).
\end{equation*}
Defining the vorticity tensor $F_{ij}:=\partial_{i}A_{j}-\partial_{j}A_{i}$
and using $\dot{x}^{j}F_{ji}=\dot{x}^{j}\epsilon_{ijk}(\nabla\times
\Apartition)_{k}$, the last term becomes
$\mu(\dot{\mathbf{x}}\times(\nabla\times\Apartition))_{i}$.
In vector form:
\begin{equation}
  \mu\ddot{\mathbf{x}}
    = -\nabla\Depth + \mu\dot{\mathbf{x}}\times(\nabla\times\Apartition),
\end{equation}
reproducing~\eqref{eq:partitionel}. \qed

\subsection{Beyond the Lorentz-Force Shape}
\label{subsec:beyondlorentz}
% ============================================================

The equation of motion~\eqref{eq:partitionel} has the Lorentz-force shape.
At lowest order this is structurally equivalent to classical electromagnetism,
but the interpretation differs: $\Depth$ is the partition depth (a scalar
potential for the partition structure, not the electrostatic potential
\textit{per se}); $\Apartition$ is the partition vector potential (a vector
potential of the partition flow, not the magnetic vector potential).

In the limit where $\Depth$ reduces to the electrostatic potential $\Phi$
and $\Apartition$ reduces to the magnetic vector potential, the partition
Lagrangian reduces exactly to the Lagrangian of a charged particle in an
electromagnetic field.
This reduction requires identifying partition boundaries with charge sources,
which is established in Chapter~\ref{ch:charge}.

Away from this limit, the partition Lagrangian governs dynamics over other
partition structures.
In particular, the gravitational equation of motion (geodesic in spacetime)
can in principle be derived from a partition depth field of appropriate
structure; the derivation requires extending the Lagrangian to its
general-relativistic form and is beyond the scope of this monograph.

\section{Noether's Theorem and the Conservation Laws}
\label{sec:noether}
% ============================================================

\begin{proposition}[Noether charge]
\label{prop:noether}
If the partition depth field $\Depth$ and vector potential $\Apartition$ are
invariant under a continuous symmetry group $G$, there exists a conserved
quantity $Q_{G}$ along every trajectory of~\eqref{eq:partitionel}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
This is Noether's theorem applied to the Lagrangian~\eqref{eq:partitionlagrangian}.
For each continuous symmetry of $\Lagr_{\Depth}$, the associated conserved
current has time-integrated charge $Q_{G}$.
Translational invariance of $\Depth$ along a direction gives momentum
conservation; rotational invariance gives angular-momentum conservation;
time-translation invariance of $\Depth$ gives energy conservation.
Each conservation law is a consequence of the symmetry of the partition depth
field along the relevant direction.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Momentum conservation]
\label{cor:momcon}
If the partition depth field $\Depth$ is translation-invariant along a
direction $\hat{\mathbf{n}}$, the component $\mathbf{p}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{n}}$
of the canonical momentum is conserved along every trajectory.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Direct application of Proposition~\ref{prop:noether} to the translation
subgroup of the Lagrangian's symmetry group.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Angular-momentum conservation]
\label{cor:angmom}
If the partition depth field $\Depth$ is rotation-invariant about an axis
$\hat{\mathbf{n}}$, the component $\mathbf{L}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{n}}$ of the
canonical angular momentum is conserved.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Direct application of Proposition~\ref{prop:noether} to the rotation
subgroup of the Lagrangian's symmetry group.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Energy conservation]
\label{cor:encon}
If $\Depth$ is time-independent, the Hamiltonian derived from $\Lagr_{\Depth}$
is a conserved quantity along trajectories.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The Legendre transform of $\Lagr_{\Depth}$ gives the Hamiltonian
\begin{equation*}
  H_{\Depth}
    = \mathbf{p}\cdot\dot{\mathbf{x}} - \Lagr_{\Depth}
    = \frac{|\mathbf{p}-\mu\Apartition|^{2}}{2\mu} + \Depth(\mathbf{x}),
\end{equation*}
where the canonical momentum is
$\mathbf{p}=\partial\Lagr_{\Depth}/\partial\dot{\mathbf{x}}
  =\mu\dot{\mathbf{x}}+\mu\Apartition$.
Time-independence of $\Depth$ and $\Apartition$ gives
$dH_{\Depth}/dt=0$ along trajectories.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Emergence of Hamiltonian mechanics]
Corollaries~\ref{cor:momcon}--\ref{cor:encon} show that the familiar
conservation laws of Hamiltonian mechanics emerge from Axiom~\ref{ax:bpsl}
via the partition Lagrangian.
Momentum, angular momentum, and energy are not postulated quantities; they
are Noether charges of the partition-depth symmetries.
When the partition depth field has the full Euclidean symmetry (translation
in three directions, rotation about three axes, time translation), the
complete set of ten conserved quantities of Galilean mechanics is recovered.
Newton's second law in the force-free case ($\nabla\Depth=0$,
$\Apartition=0$) is the statement that a partition label carrier moves
on a straight line at constant velocity --- not a law of nature, but a
consequence of zero partition-depth gradient.
\end{remark}

\section{Partition Spatial and Temporal Resolution}
\label{sec:resolution}
% ============================================================

The partition Lagrangian depends on two geometric scales of the partition
hierarchy that will recur throughout Part~II.
We define them here for reference.

\begin{definition}[Partition spatial resolution]
\label{def:partitionspatial}
At partition depth $n$, the \emph{spatial resolution} $\ell_{n}$ is defined
by
\[
  \ell_{n} := \mu(\Omega)^{1/3}\cdot b^{-n/3},
\]
where $b$ is the branching base of the partition tree.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Partition temporal resolution]
\label{def:partitiontemporal}
At partition depth $n$, the \emph{temporal resolution} $\taup^{(n)}$ is the
inverse of the maximum Koopman frequency $\omega_{n,\max}$ accessible to
observations at depth $n$:
\[
  \taup^{(n)} := 2\pi/\omega_{n,\max}.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[The partition lag in dynamics]
The temporal resolution $\taup^{(n)}$ at depth $n$ is the \emph{partition
lag}: the minimum time required to complete a categorical distinction at that
depth.
Partition lag plays a role in the partition Lagrangian because the
velocity $\dot{\mathbf{x}}$ is the ratio of spatial displacement to temporal
displacement; at depth $n$, this ratio is bounded by
$\ell_{n}/\taup^{(n)}=\ell_{n}\omega_{n,\max}/(2\pi)$.
The universality of this bound across all depths is the origin of the
propagation bound $c$, derived in Chapter~\ref{ch:lorentz}.
\end{remark}

\section{Partition Lag and Undetermined Residue}
\label{sec:partitionlag}
% ============================================================

Every partition operation takes a finite time $\taup$ to complete.
During that interval, the system continues to evolve, generating a portion
of its trajectory that is never assigned a partition coordinate.
This \emph{undetermined residue} has far-reaching consequences.

\begin{consequence}{Minimum Partition Lag}{minlag}
For any partition operation refining a Koopman mode of energy uncertainty
$\Delta E$, the partition lag satisfies
\begin{equation}
  \taup \geq \frac{\hbar}{\Delta E}.
  \label{eq:minlag}
\end{equation}
\end{consequence}

\begin{proof}
The partition operation is a measurement that distinguishes states differing
in energy by at most $\Delta E$.
By the time--energy uncertainty relation, the operation cannot complete in a
time shorter than $\hbar/\Delta E$.
\end{proof}

\begin{consequence}{Residue Generation}{residuegen}
Let a bounded oscillator with state-evolution rate $|d|\psi\rangle/dt|=v$
undergo a partition operation of duration $\taup$.
The state at partition completion differs from the state at partition
initiation by
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{R}
    = \int_{t_{0}}^{t_{0}+\taup}\frac{d|\psi\rangle}{dt}\,dt
  \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
  |\mathcal{R}| \geq v\cdot\taup > 0,
  \label{eq:residue}
\end{equation}
for any non-static system.
The quantity $\mathcal{R}$ is the \emph{undetermined residue}: the portion
of the system that existed at $t_{0}$ but moved before partition completion
at $t_{0}+\taup$, and was therefore never assigned a partition coordinate.
\end{consequence}

\begin{proof}
Direct integration of the state-evolution equation over the partition-lag
interval gives the displacement.
By Consequence~\ref{cons:oscillatory}, $v=0$ would imply static dynamics,
contradicting Axiom~\ref{ax:bpsl} for non-trivial systems.
Hence $|\mathcal{R}|>0$ for every partition operation acting on a bounded
oscillator.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The status of residue]
Residue is neither absent (it existed at $t_{0}$) nor present in any
actualised partition cell (it moved before completion).
It is undetermined in the strict sense that no partition coordinate
$(n,l,m,s)$ is assigned to it.
Properties requiring partition --- charge, position, identity --- are
therefore undefined for residue.
Residue retains dynamical influence through the partition-depth field
$\Depth$ but is not directly observable via any partition-based measurement.
\end{remark}

\begin{consequence}{Cumulative Residue}{cumulativeresidue}
For a sequence of $k$ independent partition operations on a bounded
oscillator with characteristic evolution rate $v$,
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{total}}
    = \sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathcal{R}_{i}
    = k\,v\,\taup.
  \label{eq:cumulativeresidue}
\end{equation}
\end{consequence}

\begin{consequence}{Residue Fraction per Operation}{residuefraction}
For a partition operation in $d$-dimensional partition space with branching
factor $b$, the fraction of phase-space volume that becomes residue (rather
than actualised partition) is
\begin{equation}
  f_{\mathcal{R}} = \frac{b^{d}-1}{b^{d}}.
  \label{eq:residuefrac}
\end{equation}
For $b=3$, $d=3$ (the partition base and spatial dimension),
$f_{\mathcal{R}}=26/27\approx 0.963$.
\end{consequence}

\begin{proof}
At each partition level, the categorical volume divides into $b^{d}$ cells.
By the geometry of the partition operation, exactly one cell becomes the
actualised partition target; the remaining $b^{d}-1$ cells become residue
because the system evolves into them during the lag interval $\taup$ before
they are categorically resolved.
The fraction is $(b^{d}-1)/b^{d}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Three system components]
\label{rem:threecomponents}
Combining Consequences~\ref{cons:residuegen}, \ref{cons:cumulativeresidue},
and~\ref{cons:residuefraction}, every bounded system at any time decomposes
into three mutually exclusive and exhaustive components:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep, label=(\roman*)]
  \item \textbf{Actualised partitions} $\mathcal{A}$: fully partitioned
        structures with complete partition coordinates;
  \item \textbf{Accumulated residue} $\mathcal{R}$: undetermined portions
        from partition lag, lacking partition coordinates;
  \item \textbf{Partition potential} $\Pi$: unrealised capacity for further
        partition operations.
\end{enumerate}
The total content is conserved: $\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{R}+\Pi=\mathrm{const}$.
This decomposition recurs in later chapters: in Chapter~\ref{ch:mass},
accumulated residue is identified with inertial mass; the cosmological
dark-to-ordinary matter ratio can in principle be derived from the residue
fraction $f_{\mathcal{R}}$ of Consequence~\ref{cons:residuefraction}.
\end{remark}

\section{Force-Free Ion Trajectories in Mass Spectrometry}
\label{sec:forcefree-ion}
% ============================================================

The partition Lagrangian has direct application to the mass spectrometric
setting that motivates this monograph.

\begin{remark}[Gradient descent in the instrument]
In the mass spectrometric context (Part~VIII), the partition Lagrangian
means that an ion in an instrument does not experience forces in the
traditional sense.
It follows the path of steepest descent on the partition depth landscape
$\Depth(\mathbf{x},t)$ created by the instrument's field configuration
(electric sector, magnetic sector, rf quadrupole, time-of-flight drift
region).
The ion trajectory is geometrically determined by the partition structure.

The GPU implementation (Chapter~\ref{ch:gpu}) uses exactly this gradient
descent formulation: the shader pipeline computes $-\nabla\Depth$ at each
step, making the hardware a physical observable of the partition depth field.
Each fragment shader evaluation is a discrete partition operation; the
accumulated render output is the actualised partition of the ion's
trajectory.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Newton's second law as a special case]
Newton's second law $m\ddot{\mathbf{x}}=\mathbf{F}$ is a special case of
the partition equation of motion~\eqref{eq:partitionel} in the limit where:
(a) the vector potential $\Apartition=0$ (no vorticity of the partition
flow); and (b) the partition depth gradient $-\nabla\Depth$ is identified
with the Newtonian force $\mathbf{F}$.
In this limit, the partition Lagrangian reduces to the standard mechanical
Lagrangian $\Lagr=T-V$ with $V=\Depth$.
Newton's second law is thus a consequence of the Axiom, not a foundational
postulate.
\end{remark}
