\section{Continuous Embedding and Virtual Substates}

A finite partition hierarchy can be embedded in a continuous space via the
Fisher metric.  The embedding reveals that between any two adjacent partition
cells there exist intermediate coordinates --- \emph{virtual substates} ---
that are not themselves observable partition cells but are necessary for the
geometry of the embedding.

\begin{consequence}{Continuous Embedding}{continuousembed}
  The partition algebra $\mathcal{A}_\infty$ embeds isometrically in the
  space of probability distributions on $\Omega$ via the Fisher--Rao metric.
  This embedding is unique up to reparametrisation (Chentsov's theorem).
\end{consequence}

\begin{consequence}{Virtual Substates}{virtualsubstates}
  In the Fisher-metric embedding, the geodesic between two adjacent partition
  cells passes through intermediate points that are not cells of any
  $\Partition_n$.  These intermediate points are \emph{virtual substates}:
  they have definite coordinates in the embedding space but no realisation as
  measurable partition cells.
\end{consequence}

\section{Sub-Nucleon Structure as Path Opacity}

\begin{consequence}{Sub-Nucleon Virtual Structure}{subnucleon}
  The constituents of a nucleon (quarks) are virtual substates in the ternary
  recursive decomposition of the nucleon's partition cell.
  They are \emph{path-opaque}: the specific sub-coordinate path through the
  Fisher-metric embedding is not distinguishable in the final global state.
  Therefore, quarks cannot be isolated as global partition states.
\end{consequence}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
  In the ternary partition ($b=3$), each nucleon cell decomposes into three
  virtual sub-cells at each level.
  The global state is the coset of the sub-cell decomposition, which collapses
  all path information.
  An isolated quark would require fixing the sub-cell path, which is
  path-opaque by the embedding structure.
  QCD confinement is the dynamical realisation of this structural fact.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
  QCD asymptotic freedom and lattice-QCD confinement are the computational
  realisations of path opacity.
  Both statements are correct; they are complementary.
  The partition framework supplies the \emph{reason} for confinement
  (structural path opacity) that QCD supplies computationally.
\end{remark}

\section{Fractional Charges}
\label{sec:ch12-fractional}

Quarks carry charges $\pm\frac{1}{3}e$ and $\pm\frac{2}{3}e$.
In the partition framework, these are fractional boundary crossings of virtual
substates.
Full boundary crossings give integer charge (proton: $e$, neutron: $0$).
The virtual boundary crossing gives fractional charge confined to the path-opaque
interior.
