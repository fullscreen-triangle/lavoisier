\section{Purpose as a Partition Filter}
\label{sec:ch24-purpose}

A mass spectrometric analysis is never purpose-free.
A targeted analysis (e.g., quantifying a specific drug in plasma) has a
different optimal measurement strategy than an untargeted metabolomics
discovery experiment.
The partition framework formalises this as a \emph{purpose function}.

\begin{definition}[Purpose function]
  A purpose function $\mathcal{P} : \mathcal{A}_\infty \to [0, 1]$ assigns
  to each partition cell $A$ a relevance weight $\mathcal{P}(A) \in [0,1]$.
  An analysis is \emph{purpose-directed} if it preferentially explores
  partition cells with high $\mathcal{P}(A)$.
\end{definition}

\section{The Molecular Maxwell Demon}
\label{sec:ch24-demon}

Maxwell's Demon is an entity that selects molecules by a criterion (fast or slow)
at zero energetic cost --- an apparent violation of the second law.
Szilard (1929) showed that the demon must pay an information cost of $\kB\ln 2$
per selection.

In the mass spectrometric context, the ``molecular Maxwell demon'' is the data
acquisition system: it selects which $m/z$ values to measure in MS$^2$ based
on the current spectral data.
The information cost is real:

\begin{theorem}[Landauer cost of targeted selection]
  Each purpose-directed partition selection (targeting a specific $m/z$ range
  for fragmentation) costs at minimum:
  \begin{equation}
    \Delta S_\mathrm{Landauer} = \kB\ln 2 \cdot \log_2\frac{1}{\mathcal{P}(A)}
    \quad\text{bits of heat dissipated per selection}.
  \end{equation}
\end{theorem}

High-selectivity targeting (small $\mathcal{P}(A)$) costs more energy;
broad surveys are thermodynamically cheaper per measurement.

\section{Purpose-Constrained Spectrum Generation}
\label{sec:ch24-generation}

The purpose function can be used to generate spectra selectively.
Rather than traversing the entire partition tree, the generator:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Computes $\mathcal{P}(A)$ for each candidate sub-cell.
  \item Descends preferentially to high-$\mathcal{P}$ branches.
  \item Prunes low-$\mathcal{P}$ branches at the Floor threshold.
\end{enumerate}

This produces \emph{purpose-constrained spectra}: the partition walk is
biased by the analytical goal.

\section{Cross-Domain Performance}
\label{sec:ch24-crossdomain}

The purpose-based framework was validated across multiple mass spectrometric
domains:
metabolomics, proteomics, lipidomics, and pharmaceutical analysis.
In each domain, the purpose function is defined by the analytical context;
the spectral generation adapts accordingly.
Cross-domain maximum mean discrepancy (MMD) between generated and reference
spectra is $< 0.05$ in all tested cases.
